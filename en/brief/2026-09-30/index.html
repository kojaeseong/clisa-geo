Kim Yo Jong claims DMZ mine blast was staged by Seoul

On September 29, 2026, the Joint Chiefs of Staff said in a statement issued in the name of its vice chairman, Kwon Dae-won, that debris from the September 21 blast site contained the same components as North Korean mines, that responsibility lay entirely with the North, and that the military would take corresponding measures. On September 30, Kim Yo Jong, department director of the Workers' Party Central Committee, called this "a clear farce" in a statement carried by the Korean Central News Agency (KCNA), warning that "ringleaders of provocation should be prepared for paying price."

Bearing on the assessmentsWith no channel through which the two Koreas could jointly verify the incident, the dispute over responsibility is turning directly into threats. That raises the forecast that inter-Korean military hotlines will remain unrestored, and it runs opposite to the conditions for managed coexistence (restored hotlines and reunions of separated families under Korean Peninsula C1).

Russian drone strike sets Kyiv's Academy of Sciences ablaze

A Russian drone attack on September 29 set fire to the building of the National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine in Kyiv, killing at least two people; Zelensky said nine people were killed nationwide that day. The same day, the Ukrainian outlet LIGA.net, citing The Atlantic, reported that Washington was pushing for U.S.-Russia-Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi in October and saw a halt to mutual strikes on energy facilities as a possible point of agreement.

Bearing on the assessmentsIf trilateral talks take place in October, the forecast that U.S.-Russia-Ukraine technical-level talks will be held resolves yes, so the report raises that forecast. But because the agenda is a partial deal such as a halt to strikes on energy facilities rather than a full ceasefire, the assessment that the war of attrition will continue (Russia-Ukraine C1) stands.

U.S. sanctions Iran's arms procurement network even as it talks of renewed negotiations

Trump said on September 28 that he expected negotiations with Iran to resume this week, and Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said, "We stand ready for diplomacy. It is up to President Trump to choose." On September 29, the U.S. Treasury Department sanctioned 10 targets in Iran's military procurement network spanning China, Hong Kong, Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Iran.

Bearing on the assessmentsBoth sides speak of dialogue, but Washington is not easing sanctions, so there is still no basis to conclude that a phased agreement that first trades the reopening of the strait for the lifting of the blockade (Iran C7) is getting closer.

Hormuz traffic rises, but Brent tops $105 a barrel

U.S. Energy Secretary Chris Wright said on September 28 that about 13 million barrels of crude a day were passing through the strait. Yet Brent rose for a second straight day on September 29, to $105.91 a barrel. Iran's supreme leader, Mojtaba Khamenei, claimed that U.S. forces had taken hits and could advance no further.

Bearing on the assessmentsThe picture resembles the incomplete reopening described in Iran C1, in which the strait partly reopens without a formal end to the war. With oil holding above $100, it weighs against the forecast that Brent will end the year below $90.
