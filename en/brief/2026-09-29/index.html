Investigation finds DMZ blast very likely caused by North Korean mine

The Joint Chiefs of Staff announced on September 28 that an explosion on September 21 south of the Military Demarcation Line in the DMZ on the western front, which injured three soldiers, was very likely caused by a North Korean mine. A final determination will follow a further investigation with the United Nations Command; it is the first incident of its kind since the wooden-box mine incident of August 2015.

Bearing on the assessmentsThe incident supports the forecast that inter-Korean military hotlines will remain unrestored. In 2015, the resumption of loudspeaker broadcasts toward the North and an exchange of artillery fire were followed by high-level inter-Korean talks that produced the August 25 agreement; with the North now rejecting inter-Korean dialogue altogether, the same path is unlikely to open.

North Korea declares at the U.N. that its nuclear status is irreversible

Speaking in the U.N. General Assembly's general debate on September 28 (New York time), North Korean Vice Foreign Minister Kim Son Gyong said the country's status as a nuclear weapons state could not be reversed under any circumstances. The speech denounced South Korea-U.S. cooperation on nuclear-powered submarines but made no mention of talks with Trump, and the next day North Korea's Foreign Ministry issued a memorandum condemning the approval of nuclear-powered submarines for South Korea.

Bearing on the assessmentsThe speech fits the assessment that the North's best move is a direct deal with Washington that secures de facto recognition of its nuclear status (Korean Peninsula C7). The absence of any reference to talks is not, on its own, treated as a signal to change the forecast of a Trump-Kim summit.

U.S. demands that nuclear issue be addressed in indirect talks with Iran

The United States and Iran continued indirect talks in New York, each meeting separately with Qatari mediators. A U.S. official said any agreement must address the nuclear issue, adding that Iran had signaled flexibility but the two sides differed over timing. Iran's chief negotiator, parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, warned that if Iran cannot sell its oil, no one will.

Bearing on the assessmentsThat weighs against the forecast of a phased agreement that reopens the strait and lifts the blockade first and defers the nuclear issue to a later stage. Ghalibaf's warning raises the risk of escalation across the entire Gulf (Iran C2), the outcome that would cost South Korea the most.

Russia sets its biggest defense spending increase since the invasion for 2027

According to Russian government budget documents obtained by Reuters, defense spending in 2027 will total 17.1 trillion rubles, about 27 percent more than planned last year. Putin signed a decree on September 28 increasing the military's authorized strength by 15,500, the fourth increase this year. Zelensky claimed North Korea was preparing to send about 10,000 more troops.

Bearing on the assessmentsThis supports the assessment that a war of attrition lasting through 2030 is the most likely outcome (Russia-Ukraine C1) and weighs against the forecasts of a full ceasefire taking effect and of a ceasefire holding. The additional North Korean deployment is a Ukrainian claim and has not been independently confirmed.

China Coast Guard announces "routine law-enforcement patrols" east of Taiwan

The China Coast Guard announced on September 29 that a formation of its ships was conducting routine law-enforcement patrols in waters east of Taiwan, and said it had also been patrolling waters near Kinmen since early September. Taiwan's Coast Guard Administration called the patrols "fake law enforcement, real intrusion" and sent vessels to monitor them.

Bearing on the assessmentsThe move extends China's gray-zone pressure, asserting jurisdiction without the use of force. But boarding inspections of vessels and controls on passage have not yet been confirmed, so it is not yet enough to change the related forecasts and is treated only as an upward factor.

U.S. and China agree to keep discussing further extension of tariff truce

Briefing on the outcome of the eighth round of U.S.-China economic and trade consultations on September 28, China's Ministry of Commerce said that after extending the tariff truce to January 10, 2027, the two sides had agreed to keep discussing a further extension. Each country published a list of goods worth about $30 billion slated for tariff cuts.

Bearing on the assessmentsThis favors the forecast that by January 10, 2027, the truce will be extended again or replaced with a longer agreement.
