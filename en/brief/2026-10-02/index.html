Trump says stepping up Iran strikes after midterms is "possible"

In a Time magazine interview published on October 1, 2026, Trump said it was "possible" when asked whether he could increase the bombing of Iran after the November midterm elections. The same day, a U.S. official told Axios that Secretary of State Marco Rubio had ordered Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi's delegation, then in New York, to leave the country; Iran denied this, saying the delegation had left on a prearranged schedule.

Bearing on the assessmentsRight after Iran received the U.S. reply, Washington sent its delegation home and raised the prospect of wider strikes, a sign that the phased exchange described in Conclusion 7 of the Iran and Hormuz analysis is unlikely to lead to a signing soon. It raises the forecast that the U.S. will resume airstrikes on Iran's mainland and lowers the forecast that the U.S. and Iran will sign a phased agreement.

Lee urges North Korea to restore dialogue in Armed Forces Day speech

At the Armed Forces Day ceremony on October 1, 2026, President Lee Jae-myung said, "We will consistently pursue practical measures to lower military tensions between the South and the North," adding, "I hope and urge that the North will now join us on the path to restoring trust and reviving severed dialogue." He referred to the soldiers injured in the DMZ explosion only as victims of an unforeseen accident, and pledged to regain wartime operational control (OPCON).

Bearing on the assessmentsThe government has held to its line of restoring dialogue even after the defense minister flatly declared that the North had violated the Armistice Agreement, in effect reaching for the conditions that would change Conclusion 1 of the Korean Peninsula analysis (restored military hotlines and reunions of separated families). With no answering move from the North, the forecast that inter-Korean military hotlines will remain unrestored does not move.

Russia warns it would use "all its arsenal, including nuclear weapons" if Kaliningrad is blockaded

In a diplomatic note sent to NATO member states on September 30, 2026, Russia claimed to have information that NATO was preparing an air and naval blockade of Kaliningrad and warned that, if a blockade were imposed, it was ready to use "all its arsenal, including nuclear weapons." At a Valdai Discussion Club meeting in Moscow on October 1, President Vladimir Putin said the warning was not a threat but a response to attempts to intimidate Russia; NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte countered that Russia must stop its nuclear threats.

Bearing on the assessmentsAfter a string of provocations along NATO's eastern flank through September, Moscow has now raised nuclear use in writing. That fits Conclusion 5 of the Russia-Ukraine War analysis, under which Russia keeps dealing with Washington while increasing pressure on Europe. It has no direct bearing on the forecast that U.S.-Russia-Ukraine technical-level talks will take place.

U.S. officials see Chinese invasion of Taiwan before 2028 as unlikely

Reuters reported on October 1, 2026, citing four people including current and former U.S. officials, that Washington sees a Chinese invasion of Taiwan before 2028 as increasingly unlikely despite President Xi Jinping's order to be ready for war by 2027. The reasons cited were purges of the military leadership that have left only two of the seven members of the Central Military Commission in office, and the possibility that Beijing will wait for the outcome of Taiwan's 2028 presidential election.

Bearing on the assessmentsThe assessment points in the same direction as Conclusion 6 of the Taiwan Strait analysis: a two-track strategy in which China avoids the use of force through the January 2028 presidential election while trying to influence its outcome. The report did not address blockades or coast guard inspections, however, so the forecasts of no blockade of Taiwan's ports and of China inspecting or detaining Taiwanese vessels do not move.

Pakistan launches airstrikes on Afghanistan's Kunar and Helmand provinces

Pakistan carried out airstrikes on Afghanistan's Kunar and Helmand provinces on the night of September 30, 2026. Zabihullah Mujahid, spokesman for the Taliban government, said nine people were killed and condemned the strikes as aggression; Pakistan's Ministry of Information and Broadcasting claimed 22 militants were killed; and the U.N. Assistance Mission in Afghanistan said at least 10 civilians, most of them children, were killed.

Bearing on the assessmentsThe strikes follow the September 24 raids on 10 sites including Kandahar, and there is no sign of mediation or ceasefire talks. They lower the forecast of a formal ceasefire between Pakistan and the Taliban.
