October 19, 2026

Russian missiles and drones hit Kyiv and other regions; 1 killed, 8 injured in the capital

Russia launched 269 drones along with ballistic and cruise missiles overnight on October 18–19, Ukraine's air force said, adding that 243 drones and four missiles were shot down or neutralized and that hits were recorded at 25 locations. Russia's Defense Ministry claimed it struck a data center in Kyiv and a locomotive depot it said was used to store missiles, as well as a cargo ship at sea 130 kilometers southwest of Odesa.

WSJ: Putin proposed a three-way Ukraine summit with Trump and Xi

The Wall Street Journal reported on October 18 (U.S. time) that Russian President Vladimir Putin proposed discussing an end to the war in Ukraine with President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping at the APEC summit in Shenzhen on November 18–19, without Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said there was no clear understanding yet about the meeting or its agenda, and a senior U.S. official said Trump had not agreed but might be open to it.

Zelensky claims U.S. has cleared only some of the Patriot missiles already paid for

In a Bloomberg interview published October 18, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said Washington had approved delivery of only some of the Patriot missiles bought through PURL, the program that uses NATO allies' money to buy U.S. weapons, and that this was not a technical issue. He also claimed that steps had been taken to convince Ukraine to capitulate.

Rubio says Ukraine currently has no access to Starshield, denies ordering a speed cap

Secretary of State Marco Rubio told reporters on October 17 that Ukraine does not currently have access to Starshield, the military version of Starlink, and that its request is under discussion. He called reports that the White House ordered Starlink speeds slowed "categorically false," though Ukrainian defense officials had earlier said Starshield was working again.

North Korea-China trade hits $299 million in September, up 18.6 percent year to date

North Korea-China trade came to about $299 million in September, up from $238 million in August and the year's second-highest monthly total after April, according to China's General Administration of Customs data released October 18 and reported by NK News, a North Korea-focused outlet. By the Sankei Shimbun's count, trade from January through September totaled about $2.27 billion, up 18.6 percent from a year earlier.

St. Petersburg Gov. Alexander Beglov said on October 18 that a "massive" drone attack killed one person, injured five and set fire to an oil terminal and a fuel depot, among other sites. IRGC claims another 'noncompliant' tanker hit a mine south of Hormuz

Iran's Revolutionary Guard navy claimed early on October 19, Tehran time, that a tanker leaving the strait by an unauthorized southern route with its navigation systems switched off struck a mine, causing an explosion and fire in its engine room. It did not name the ship, and there was no independent confirmation.

Taiwan's coast guard drives off 16 Chinese ships, accuses Beijing of hybrid warfare

Taiwan's Coast Guard Administration said on October 18 it drove away 16 Chinese vessels, including coast guard ships, research vessels and fishing boats, off the main island's eastern, southern and southwestern coasts and around the Pratas Islands, adding that some had entered restricted waters. It condemned China for waging hybrid warfare at several locations at once to change the status quo unilaterally.

Pakistani security officials claimed that troops fought Afghan Taliban forces for hours at several points along the border in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province's Kurram district on October 17 and that artillery-backed units destroyed Taliban posts. French protests spiral into riots and looting, with at least 1,800 arrested

Protests by about 550,000 people across France on October 17, by the authorities' count, over rising living costs and worsening school conditions descended into arson and looting. At least 1,800 people were arrested, according to a CBS News tally; Al Jazeera, citing the Interior Ministry, put the number at about 2,400. In a televised address on October 18, President Emmanuel Macron said looters would have to pay for the damage.
