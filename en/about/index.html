A Note from the Founder

I am Jae-Seong Ko, and I built and run Clisa Geopolitics. I am South Korean. I studied economics and have followed and studied geopolitics for many years. I worked in corporate jobs and as a management consultant, and I currently manage investment assets alongside this work. I plan to devote more of my time and resources to Clisa Geopolitics in the years ahead.

Why I do this

International affairs can seem remote, but they bear directly on everyday life — prices and jobs, personal wealth, military service, retirement. Because those links are rarely visible, many people feel the effects of geopolitical shifts only belatedly, or never recognize them at all. Clisa Geopolitics tracks the major conflicts and trends and examines what they mean for South Korea and for the lives of South Koreans. I believe that the more widely people grasp how close geopolitical change is to their own lives, the more deeply policymakers will be able to engage with it. And if this site can contribute, however modestly, to how policy is made, so much the better.

I had long wanted to do geopolitical analysis, but finding the time to gather, organize, and study the sheer volume of material was hard. In early 2023, I launched an AI-assisted advisory service, but the technology of the time fell short, and I wound it down before long. As AI advanced rapidly, I concluded in 2026 that what I had long only imagined had finally become possible, and I started this site.

AI, politics, and geopolitics

I expect AI to play an ever larger role in politics and policymaking. Parties, interest groups, and governments will first use AI to bolster their arguments, then gradually come to rely on it for judgments and decisions as well. Citizens, too, will move from using AI to compare the arguments of rival camps to relying on it in forming their own views. That will magnify the influence of attempts to tilt AI’s answers through slanted questions or distorted data. Clisa Geopolitics makes extensive use of AI to check facts and to anticipate how events may unfold. In the process, I want to learn where AI is strong and where it is prone to error on questions of politics and geopolitics, and to find ways to use it well and to guard against its misuse. I hope to exchange views on this with readers.

How the site is made

I set the topics and the editorial direction; AI assists with research and drafting; and I review the facts, judgments, and wording before anything is published. For major developments and scenarios, the site publishes forecasts together with the date by which each will be resolved, so that they are not vague predictions but claims that can later be checked. Comments and advice are welcome at clisageo@clisa.ai, and I will read them with an open mind. Thank you.
