Power Structures · Armed forces

The armed forces of Israel

Command · Capabilities · Deployment · Posture changes · As of October 8, 2026

← Power Structures · IsraelIsrael's Knesset and electionsCountry profile · Israel

Israel's military answers to the government, and the defense minister exercises authority over it on the government's behalf. The strikes on Iran that Israel launched with the United States on February 28, 2026, displayed its long-range precision-strike capability and layered missile defenses, but its weak point is endurance: whether its interceptor stocks and a force that relies on reservists can hold up over time. Its troops are stretched simultaneously across southern Lebanon, more than half of the Gaza Strip, a buffer zone in southern Syria, and the West Bank.

Command and control

Basic Law: The Military (1976) places the armed forces under the government, assigns the defense minister to oversee them on the government's behalf, and provides that the government appoints the chief of staff on the defense minister's recommendation. Decisions on war, and on military action almost certain to lead to war, rest with the government (Basic Law: The Government, Article 40), which in 2018 delegated this power permanently to the security cabinet. A provision in that year's amendment allowing the prime minister and the defense minister to decide alone "in extreme circumstances" was soon struck. The Knesset's direct hold on the military is not a veto over going to war but the Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee's approval of emergency reserve call-ups, along with the budget; the Supreme Court, for its part, rules on the legal basis of military manpower, as in the conscription of Haredi (ultra-Orthodox) men.

Collective declarations by reservists refusing to serve recur in each political crisis, involving 1,000 to 10,000 people
  • The Powell & Thyne coup dataset records no coup or coup attempt in Israel, and the country has no regime-protection units. Checks on the government come from the Knesset, the Supreme Court, and coalition partners, not from the military.
  • On July 21, 2023, ahead of a vote on the judicial overhaul, about 1,000 air force reservists signed a letter warning that they would stop serving; on July 22, some 10,000 reservists from a range of units declared that they would stop volunteering for duty (Israel Hayom).
  • Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu announced the dismissal of Defense Minister Yoav Gallant on March 26, 2023, then reversed course; on November 5, 2024, he fired Gallant again and replaced him with Israel Katz (Al Jazeera).
  • On January 21, 2025, Chief of Staff Herzi Halevi announced his resignation, taking responsibility for the failure to prevent the October 7 attack. On February 1, Eyal Zamir, then director general of the Defense Ministry, was named as his successor; after government approval, he took office on March 5 as the IDF's 24th chief of staff (Jewish Press, Times of Israel).
  • In April 2025, after about 1,000 air force reservists and retirees published an advertisement calling for the hostages' return and an end to the war, the air force said that, with the chief of staff's approval, it would dismiss signatories still serving in the reserves (Al Arabiya, April 10, 2025). The military treated political dissent as a disciplinary matter.
  • On November 24, 2025, Katz ordered the defense establishment comptroller to review the dismissals and reprimands Zamir had issued over the October 7 failures and froze IDF promotions and senior appointments for 30 days; Zamir publicly countered that the moves harmed the military's readiness (Times of Israel). On July 13, 2026, Zamir wrote to the prime minister, the defense minister, and the Knesset that Basic Law: Torah Study was at odds with the military's needs.
  • On October 6, 2026, Katz warned that Israel would declare war on the Palestinian Authority if its security forces turned their guns on Israelis; according to the Jerusalem Post, his remarks had not been coordinated with the IDF. Disagreement has surfaced in public statements and letters; no case in which it led to a refusal of orders has been confirmed.

Capabilities

With 316 combat aircraft (IISS, 2025), including F-35I stealth fighters, the Israeli military can strike the Iranian mainland directly at ranges beyond 1,000 kilometers; it said it hit 500 targets inside Iran on February 28, 2026, the first day of the campaign alone. Its missile defenses are layered, from exo-atmospheric interception (Arrow 3) to Iron Dome and the Iron Beam laser, which counter rockets and drones. Its weakness is endurance. In March 2026, U.S. officials said Israel was running critically short of ballistic-missile interceptors (Israel's foreign minister denied it), and on April 6 a ministerial procurement committee approved accelerated Arrow production.

Proficiency

Proficiency · judgment estimate
High · has repeatedly conducted long-range precision strikes and multifront ground operations in combat Among the most capable in the Middle East (judgment estimate), though its ability to sustain high-intensity operations over several months is moderate, given manpower shortages and interceptor depletion
  • Since October 2023, the military has been fighting continuously for more than three years, in Gaza, Lebanon, Syria, Yemen, and Iran. In the Twelve-Day War of June 2025 and in the strikes on Iran that began on February 28, 2026, the air force hit targets more than 1,000 kilometers away on a large scale (500 targets on the first day, according to an air force statement cited by Wikipedia [Secondary]).
  • After Iran fired ballistic missiles on June 7, 2026 (11, according to Israel's ambassador to the United States), the IDF said it struck military targets in central and western Iran the next day (Axios).
  • Combat has also exposed weaknesses. The intelligence and preparedness failures that allowed the October 7, 2023, attack led to the resignations of the chief of staff and the head of Southern Command.
  • At a March 2026 cabinet meeting on Haredi conscription, Zamir reportedly said he was raising "10 red flags" before the military collapses in on itself (Jerusalem Post). Because the standing army is small, a long war depends on repeated reserve call-ups.
  • Pilots' annual flight hours are not public and could not be used as evidence.

Deployment

The Israeli military's center of gravity lies in the north. After the war with Hezbollah resumed in March 2026, five divisions, tens of thousands of troops, had entered southern Lebanon by April; the military calls the area a "forward defense zone" and has kept its forces there under a nominal ceasefire. In Gaza, it holds the territory east of the ceasefire line known as the Yellow Line and has been pushing that area outward. In Syria, it remains on the summit of Mount Hermon and in the buffer zone it seized after the Assad regime fell in December 2024. The Iranian front is covered not by ground forces but by the air force and missile defenses, so Israel's ability to respond to Iran depends on the air force's strike capacity and its interceptor stocks, separate from the manpower burden on other fronts.
