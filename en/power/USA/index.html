Conditions on cutting U.S. troops in Korea and transferring wartime operational controlThe Korean Peninsula →

Where it stands
The fiscal 2026 National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA) bars the use of funds to reduce U.S. Forces Korea below 28,500 troops or to complete the transfer of wartime operational control in a way that departs from the plan Seoul and Washington agreed. On July 22, 2026, the House passed a bill extending the same conditions through fiscal 2027, and the Senate Armed Services Committee's version would require the defense secretary's certification before the transfer could be completed at all.
The legislature's power
Through appropriations and the NDAA, Congress attaches conditions to any presidential decision to cut troops or transfer operational control. The conditions lift 60 days after the defense secretary certifies and submits an assessment.
The vote count
The House passed the bill 216–212, with 209 Republicans, six Democrats, and one independent voting yes. In the Senate, a July 14 vote to take up the bill stalled at 50–46, short of the 60 votes needed.
Positions
Both the House bill and the Senate Armed Services Committee version keep the commitment to roughly 28,500 U.S. troops in Korea among their provisions on strengthening the alliance.
Next steps and deadlines
House and Senate negotiators will settle the final text. Fiscal 2027 spending continues under a stopgap through December 11.

Floor statements for and against

The law has been enacted and the House has voted, but no floor speech for or against addressed this provision.

Taiwan provisions in the National Defense Authorization ActThe Taiwan Strait →

Where it stands
The defense authorization bill passed by the House (H.R. 8800) makes up to $1 billion available for the Taiwan Security Cooperation Initiative in fiscal 2027 (Section 1246). The Senate Armed Services Committee's bill (S. 4784) renames it the First Island Chain Security Cooperation Initiative, extends it to the Philippines, raises the cap to $1.5 billion, and extends it through the end of 2032 (Section 1233). It also lets the president transfer to Taiwan obsolete or surplus Pentagon stocks, such as ammunition and missiles, held in stockpiles in Taiwan (Section 1250).
The legislature's power
Congress sets the scope of the administration's security assistance to Taiwan through authorizations and conditions, on the foundation of the Taiwan Relations Act (1979). The Senate committee's bill requires notifying Congress 30 days before any stockpile transfer.
The vote count
The full House passed it 216 to 212 on July 22. In the Senate, a vote to take it up on the floor fell short of 60 votes, stalling it.
Next steps and deadlines
Reconciliation between the two chambers. Fiscal 2027 spending runs on a continuing resolution through December 11.

Floor statements for and against

The House held a floor vote, but no floor speech for or against addressed the Taiwan provisions.

Enforcing tariffs under the Russia sanctions lawThe Russia-Ukraine War →

Where it stands
The Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026 was signed into law on September 18, 2026. The president must impose tariffs of up to 100 percent on major buyers of Russian oil and gas by October 18.
The legislature's power
Congress made the tariffs mandatory but left the president a way to waive them in the national interest. Any lifting of sanctions on Russia is subject to congressional review.
The vote count
Senate 86–11 (August 7); House 262–159 (September 16). In the House, 152 Democrats voted no, objecting not to sanctions on Russia but to giving the president more tariff authority.
Next steps and deadlines
The October 18 deadline for imposing tariffs; whether the president grants waivers, and which countries are targeted.

Votes against the party line

  • Senate passage of H.R. 5334 · August 7, 2026 · Nine Democrats, independent Bernie Sanders and Republican Rand Paul voted no (Democrats: Lisa Blunt Rochester, Maggie Hassan, Mazie Hirono, Ed Markey, Jon Ossoff, Alex Padilla, Elizabeth Warren, Peter Welch, Ron Wyden)
  • House concurs in Senate amendment · September 16, 2026 · Most Democrats (152) voted no and 58 voted yes. Seven Republicans voted no (Warren Davidson, Andy Harris, Thomas Massie, Tom McClintock, Ralph Norman, Chip Roy, Keith Self)

The Iran war powers resolutionIran and Hormuz →

Where it stands
The House has passed three resolutions in 2026 limiting the president's military action against Iran, but the Senate rejected one, 49–50, on September 24.
The legislature's power
Congress can try to limit military action through a resolution, but overriding a presidential veto takes two-thirds of both chambers. No military action has ever been halted by such a resolution.
The vote count
Seven Republicans voted yes in the House on September 15; in the Senate on September 24, four Republicans voted yes and one Democrat voted no.
Next steps and deadlines
Another attempt at a Senate vote; the balance of seats after the November 3 midterm elections.

Votes against the party line

  • House vote on H.Con.Res. 89 · July 23, 2026 · Four Republicans voted yes (Tom Barrett, Warren Davidson, Brian Fitzpatrick, Thomas Massie)
  • House vote on H.Con.Res. 93 · September 15, 2026 · Seven Republicans voted yes (Tom Barrett, Warren Davidson, Brian Fitzpatrick, Nancy Mace, Thomas Massie, Mariannette Miller-Meeks, Zach Nunn)
  • Senate vote on H.Con.Res. 89 · September 24, 2026 · Four Republicans voted yes (Susan Collins, Lisa Murkowski, Rand Paul, Thom Tillis); one Democrat voted no (John Fetterman)

Independents are counted with the party they caucus with (the Democrats).

The MATCH Act (aligning allied controls on chipmaking equipment)AI and Chips →

Where it stands
The House Foreign Affairs Committee approved an amended version on April 22, 2026 (H.R. 8170). There has been no floor vote. A companion bill in the Senate (S. 4281), introduced on April 13, is before the Banking Committee.
The legislature's power
If enacted, the administration would have 150 days either to certify to Congress that allied countries that make chip equipment (such as Japan and the Netherlands) have matching controls on equipment for China or to submit a list of those that do not. Without that certification, it would have to extend U.S. controls directly to equipment shipped from those countries and require licenses, with a policy of denial, for servicing equipment at covered facilities in countries of concern (as introduced). The bill turns the administration's discretion into an obligation.
The vote count
The committee vote was 36 to 8; the bill has 31 cosponsors.
Next steps and deadlines
A House floor vote, or whether to fold it into the reconciled defense authorization bill.
