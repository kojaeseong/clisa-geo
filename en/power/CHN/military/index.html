Power Structures · Armed forces

The armed forces of China

Command · Capabilities · Deployment · Posture changes · As of October 8, 2026

← Power Structures · ChinaDecision-maker · Xi JinpingChina's Party and military bodies and the National People's CongressCountry profile · China

China's armed forces answer to the Central Military Commission, which runs under a chairman responsibility system and therefore rests in the hands of one man, Xi Jinping. Purges since 2023 have left only two of the seven members named to the commission in 2022 in place: Xi and Vice Chairman Zhang Shengmin. China's nuclear arsenal, at roughly 620 warheads, is growing faster than any other in the world, yet the U.S. Department of Defense judges that the People's Liberation Army still lacks the lift for an amphibious invasion of Taiwan. The force's center of gravity lies in the Eastern Theater Command, responsible for Taiwan, and the Southern Theater Command, responsible for the South China Sea. In 2026, Beijing has kept up the pressure around Taiwan by scaling back military air activity and sending more coast guard vessels instead.

Command and control

The Chinese military is the Party's army before it is the state's. The general program of the Communist Party's constitution commits the Party to "absolute leadership" over the People's Liberation Army, and both Article 93 of the state constitution and Article 23 of the Party constitution vest command of the nation's armed forces in a Central Military Commission (CMC) run under a chairman responsibility system. The People's Armed Police, and the coast guard that sits within it, also take orders from the Party Central Committee and the CMC, so Xi controls the regular military and the entire paramilitary apparatus through a single chain of command. The military's internal checks, political commissars and the discipline inspection system, do not restrain the decision-maker; they serve as the instruments of his purges.

Commander in chiefXi Jinping, chairman of the Party and state Central Military Commissions. The CMC operates under a chairman responsibility system (Constitution, Article 93; Party Constitution, Article 23); the Party exercises "absolute leadership" over the military (Party Constitution, General Program)
War and deployment decisionsThe National People's Congress (NPC) decides questions of war and peace (Constitution, Article 62(15)). If the country comes under armed attack while the NPC is not in session, the NPC Standing Committee decides to declare a state of war and the president proclaims it (Articles 67(19) and 80). The use of "non-peaceful means" against Taiwan is decided by the State Council and the CMC and reported to the Standing Committee afterward (Anti-Secession Law, Article 8)
Mobilization and emergenciesOn the proposal of the State Council and the CMC, the NPC Standing Committee decides on general or partial mobilization, and the president issues the mobilization order (Constitution, Articles 67(20) and 80; National Defense Mobilization Law, Articles 13 and 14; amended law in force since October 1, 2026). China has a state-of-emergency system in place of martial law: the Standing Committee decides for an entire province or larger area (Article 67(21)), the State Council for parts of a province (Article 89(16))
Regime protection and paramilitary forcesThe People's Armed Police is under the centralized and unified leadership of the Party Central Committee and the CMC (People's Armed Police Law, Article 2); the coast guard belongs to the People's Armed Police (Article 9 of the same law). Rather than an independent praetorian guard that protects the decision-maker apart from the regular military, the system binds the entire armed forces into the Party's organization
Institutional checksFormally, the NPC Standing Committee, which votes on removing CMC members and on mobilization decisions, but there is no record of it blocking anything by vote. Inside the military, the Political Work Department, the Commission for Discipline Inspection, and political commissars at every level carry out the Party's command (Party Constitution, Article 24)
Nuclear release authorityNo procedure is set out in law or public documents. China has long declared a no-first-use policy, and under the chairman responsibility system the decision appears to rest with Xi (judgment estimate)

Does the military follow the decision-maker?

Loyalty · judgment estimate
Follows the decision-maker (Xi Jinping) · organized defection very unlikely Probability of organized defection or a coup within the next year: below 1% to 3% (judgment estimate)
  • The Powell & Thyne coup dataset (September 2025 edition) records no coup attempts in China since 1950.
  • Party committees and political commissars run alongside the military chain of command at every level (Party Constitution, Article 24), and the People's Armed Police and the coast guard are likewise under the centralized and unified leadership of the Party Central Committee and the CMC (People's Armed Police Law, Article 2). Instead of keeping separate units to counterbalance the military, the Party binds the entire force to itself directly.
  • The purges have gone ahead without visible resistance. On July 31, 2023, Beijing promoted Wang Houbin and Xu Xisheng to full general as the Rocket Force's commander and political commissar, making public the replacement of the force's leadership (People's Daily). On October 24, 2023, the NPC Standing Committee removed Defense Minister Li Shangfu. On October 17, 2025, nine senior generals were expelled from the Party and the military, among them CMC Vice Chairman He Weidong, former CMC Political Work Department Director Miao Hua, former Eastern Theater Commander Lin Xiangyang, former Rocket Force Commander Wang Houbin, and former People's Armed Police Commander Wang Chunning (Xinhua).
  • On January 24, 2026, the Party Central Committee decided to place CMC Vice Chairman Zhang Youxia and Liu Zhenli, chief of the Joint Staff Department, under investigation (Xinhua). On September 21, it announced that both had been expelled from the Party and that the CMC had earlier decided to expel them from the military (Xinhua). Among the charges, the announcement said they had been "disloyal and dishonest to the Party." This demonstrates Xi's grip on the military while also signaling his deep distrust of its top command.
  • CSIS (February 24, 2026) counted 101 senior officers at the CMC and theater-command levels who have been removed or have disappeared from view since 2022. A vacuum in command appears to be a more realistic danger than defection.

Capabilities

China fields roughly 2.03 million active-duty personnel, and its defense spending, which SIPRI estimates at $336 billion for 2025, is second only to that of the United States. The official 2026 defense budget (central government level) is 1.91 trillion yuan, up 7 percent from the previous year's actual outlays. The U.S. Department of Defense estimates that China's warhead stockpile reached the low 600s by 2024 and will exceed 1,000 by 2030, but it assesses that the PLA lacks the conventional sealift and airlift for a large-scale amphibious landing on Taiwan. Beijing's options against Taiwan that are executable today therefore appear to lie closer to blockade and firepower strikes than to invasion.

Proficiency

Proficiency · judgment estimate
Blockade and firepower strikes against Taiwan are feasible; large-scale joint amphibious operations remain a near-term weakness Blockade and firepower-strike proficiency moderate to high; joint amphibious operations low to moderate (judgment estimate)
  • China has had no large-scale combat experience since the 1979 Sino-Vietnamese War, and the June 2020 Galwan clash was a border skirmish fought without firearms. Annual pilot flight hours are not disclosed.
  • Since the August 2022 drills, the PLA has staged five more named large-scale joint exercises around Taiwan, from Joint Sword in 2023 to Justice Mission-2025. In Justice Mission-2025, it committed a Type 075 amphibious assault ship to a joint exercise for the first time and landed 10 rockets inside Taiwan's contiguous zone (Taiwan Ministry of National Defense, Taiwan Security Monitor).
  • The U.S. Department of Defense judged that the corruption investigations "very likely risk short term disruptions in the operational effectiveness of the PLA" (December 2025).
  • CSIS found that the Eastern Theater Command was without a commander for nine months, from March 2025 until Yang Zhibin's appointment on December 22, and that the interval between a triggering event and an encirclement exercise around Taiwan lengthened from three to four days in 2024 to 12 to 19 days in 2025 (February 2026).

Deployment

The PLA's center of gravity is the Eastern Theater Command, which covers Taiwan, and the Southern Theater Command, which covers the South China Sea. Group army rocket artillery brigades in Fujian Province, across the strait from Taiwan, conducted live fire during Justice Mission-2025, and the Fujian, China's third aircraft carrier, was commissioned at Sanya on Hainan Island. In 2026, with military air activity reduced, coast guard vessels have taken over the pressure campaign, patrolling continuously in the waters east of Taiwan and around Kinmen and the Pratas Islands. China's only official overseas base is its support base in Djibouti, where an expansion of signals intelligence facilities was reported in September 2026.
