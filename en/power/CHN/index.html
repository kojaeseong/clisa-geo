Based on the seven members elected in October 2022. Only Xi Jinping (chairman) and Zhang Shengmin (vice chairman) remain

The seven members of the Politburo Standing Committee are unchanged since 2022, but three of the Politburo's 24 members have been expelled from the Party, and only two of the Central Military Commission's seven remain. For the NPC's March 2026 session, 2,878 deputies were eligible to attend, down from 2,977 when the roster was confirmed in 2023. Because NPC votes ratify conclusions the Party has already reached, real change comes through personnel decisions at Central Committee plenums and Party congresses.

Votes needed

Constitutional amendments (NPC)Proposed by the NPC Standing Committee or at least one-fifth of deputies; adopted by at least two-thirds of all deputies
Laws and other bills (NPC)A majority of all deputies
Convening a Party congress earlyWhen the Central Committee deems it necessary or at least one-third of provincial-level Party organizations request it (Party Constitution, Article 19)
Non-peaceful means against TaiwanDecided by the State Council and the Central Military Commission; reported afterward to the NPC Standing Committee (Anti-Secession Law, Article 8)

Key players in the Party and the military

Foreign-policy decisions come from the seven members of the Politburo Standing Committee and the Party central commissions chaired by Xi. The other Standing Committee members each run one institution — the State Council, the NPC, the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference (CPPCC), the General Office of the Central Committee, or the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection — in roles closer to implementation than decision-making. In the military, the two remaining members of the Central Military Commission sit at the top of the chain of command.

No designated successor was elevated to the Standing Committee in 2022, and the Fourth Plenum in October 2025 merely promoted Zhang Shengmin to vice chairman without filling the vacancies. Who fills those seats at the Fifth Plenum and the 21st Party Congress is likely to be the most direct signal of the balance of power after Xi.
