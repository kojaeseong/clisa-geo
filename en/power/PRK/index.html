North Korea's Party and military bodies and the Supreme People's Assembly

Ninth Central Committee (elected February 2026) · 15th Supreme People's Assembly (elected March 2026) · As of October 6, 2026

The current balance of power

Because no deliberative body blocks decisions by vote, the distribution of seats means little. Real power is concentrated in the five-member Presidium of the Politburo — Kim Jong Un, Pak Thae Song, Jo Yong Won, Kim Jae Ryong, and Ri Il Hwan — and in the State Affairs Commission. On March 22, 2026, the first session of the 15th Supreme People's Assembly reelected Kim chairman of the State Affairs Commission and elected Jo Yong Won chairman of the Assembly's Standing Committee and first vice chairman of the State Affairs Commission.

Votes needed

Electing the Party general secretaryParty congress (every five years); Kim reelected at the Ninth Party Congress in February 2026
Electing the chairman of the State Affairs CommissionSupreme People's Assembly; Kim reelected in March 2026, with the power of recall removed by the revised constitution
Amending the constitutionAt least two-thirds of all deputies to the Supreme People's Assembly (under the previous constitution)
Use of nuclear forcesChairman of the State Affairs Commission (may be delegated to the state nuclear forces command body; 2026 constitution, Article 89)

Key players in the Party and the military

The Ninth Party Congress, held February 19–25, 2026, is regarded as having further consolidated Kim Jong Un's power through a sweeping generational turnover that cleared out veterans and returned Kim Yo Jong to the Politburo.

As a Presidium member who also chairs the Assembly's Standing Committee and serves as first vice chairman of the State Affairs Commission, Jo Yong Won is regarded as the regime's official No. 2. Kim Yo Jong, dropped from the Politburo in 2021, returned with her promotion to department director and continues to issue statements on the United States and South Korea. All of their influence derives from Kim Jong Un's trust, and no one among them appears able to block a decision independently.
