Power Structures · Armed forces

The armed forces of Ukraine

Command · Capabilities · Deployment · Posture changes · As of October 8, 2026

← Power Structures · UkraineDecision-maker · Volodymyr ZelenskyUkraine's Verkhovna RadaCountry profile · Ukraine

Ukraine's armed forces answer to President Volodymyr Zelensky, their commander-in-chief under the constitution, and since July 22, 2026, have been led by General Mykhailo Drapatyi, who replaced Oleksandr Syrskyi as commander-in-chief of the armed forces. Ukraine is rapidly expanding its domestic drone fleet and long-range strike capability, yet its defense against ballistic missiles still depends heavily on Western-supplied Patriot interceptors. The center of gravity of its forces lies in Donetsk Oblast, on the Pokrovsk and Kostiantynivka axes, where Russia is concentrating its main offensive.

Command and control

As commander-in-chief, the president appoints and dismisses the military high command and decides on the use of force, martial law, and mobilization (Article 106(17), (19), and (20) of the Constitution). The Verkhovna Rada must approve martial-law and mobilization decrees within two days, authorizes both the entry of foreign troops into Ukraine and the deployment of Ukrainian forces abroad, and votes on the appointment of the defense minister (Article 85(9), (12), (23), and (31)). On July 15, 2026, the president left Defense Minister Mykhailo Fedorov, who had repeatedly clashed with Commander-in-Chief Syrskyi, out of a cabinet reshuffle; as protests against the dismissal continued, he replaced both Syrskyi and Chief of the General Staff Andrii Hnatov on July 22. The military carried out the change of command without resistance, but the political conflict it produced fed street protests and a debate over holding elections in wartime.

Does the military follow the decision-maker?

Loyalty · judgment estimate
Follows the decision-maker · Collective defection very unlikely, though political friction over military appointments and the conduct of the war has grown Collective insubordination or coup: under 5% (central estimate about 2%); renewed public conflict over high-command appointments: moderate
  • No attempt by the military to seize power has been recorded since independence in 1991, and Article 17 of the Constitution prohibits using the armed forces to overthrow the constitutional order (English text, Constitutional Court of Ukraine).
  • The president replaced Commander-in-Chief Valerii Zaluzhnyi with Syrskyi on February 8, 2024, and Syrskyi with Drapatyi on July 22, 2026; on both occasions the military offered no public resistance. Syrskyi said he would "serve Ukraine until victory, in any position" (Office of the President announcement; NV).
  • After Defense Minister Fedorov was dismissed on July 15, 2026, protests demanding his reinstatement spread in Kyiv and other cities, and serving soldiers and veterans reported internal pressure to suppress expressions of support for him (Chatham House, July 22, 2026).
  • In an August 18, 2026, video, Fedorov accused the president of abusing martial-law powers and of corruption, and called for preparations for wartime elections. The Office of the President replied that it does not oppose elections in principle but that the question is how to hold them under air attack (Irish Times, citing Reuters; Komersant).
  • Ukraine has no separate military force dedicated to regime protection. On August 19, 2026, Khmara, a former commander of the Security Service of Ukraine (SBU) Special Operations Center who had also served as acting SBU head, was appointed defense minister (Ukrinform).

Capabilities

More than four years of full-scale war have driven Ukraine's armed forces to develop a drone-centered way of fighting at speed, and in 2026 most of the new weapon systems they adopted were produced at home. They remain dependent on Western supplies for ballistic-missile interceptors and combat aircraft, however, and manpower generation and unauthorized absence are their most serious weaknesses.

Proficiency

Proficiency · judgment estimate
Highly combat-proficient · A leader in drone warfare and long-range strike; manpower generation and command discipline are weaknesses Upper tier (battlefield adaptability: high; capacity for large-scale maneuver operations: moderate)
  • Fighting a full-scale war since February 2022, Ukraine raised the share of Russian drones it shot down or neutralized from about 85 percent in January 2026 to about 93 percent in May, and Ukrainian military observers assessed that interceptor drones accounted for more than 40 percent of kills during some of the largest attacks in late May (Institute for Science and International Security, compiling Ukrainian Air Force reports).
  • Prosecutor General's Office data show that criminal cases were opened for 235,646 instances of unauthorized absence and 53,954 of desertion between January 2022 and September 2025 (PRM, October 14, 2025).
  • After a Russian strike on a training ground on June 1, 2025, Drapatyi offered his resignation as commander of the Ground Forces, warning that "an army where no one is held accountable for losses dies from within"; since becoming commander-in-chief, he has reorganized command on the Donetsk front around two force groupings led by corps commanders (Euromaidan Press; NPR; Hromadske).
  • The Fedorov-Syrskyi dispute has been read as a clash between a drone- and technology-centered way of war and a traditional one built on infantry and artillery (Chatham House).

Deployment

The center of gravity of Ukraine's armed forces lies in Donetsk Oblast, where Russia is concentrating its main offensive. In August 2026, the president said two force groupings would be restructured to defend the region, and in the General Staff report for October 6, 2026, the Pokrovsk (27) and Kostiantynivka (14) axes again saw the most Russian assaults. Ukraine also holds the northern Kharkiv–Kupiansk and southern Zaporizhzhia fronts, while dividing rear-area resources between air defense of the capital region and long-range strikes against Russian territory and the Black Sea.
