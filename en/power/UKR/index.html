Ukraine's Verkhovna Rada

9th convocation of the Verkhovna Rada · Convened August 29, 2019 · Term extended under martial law · As of October 6, 2026

450 seats, 392 currently filled. Based on the Rada's faction roster and open data (updated October 5, 2026). Independents include the speaker and both deputy speakers.

Servant of the People clears the majority threshold by three seats, but absences and defections in its ranks mean that, in practice, measures pass only with votes from other factions. With 392 sitting members, 300 votes amounts to more than three-quarters of the sitting members, so amending the constitution after martial law ends will require the agreement of nearly every faction.

Key players in the Verkhovna Rada

The Rada's presiding officers and the ruling party's floor leader set the schedule for votes on extending martial law, ratifying treaties, and appointing ministers. Security and foreign-policy bills pass through the Committee on National Security, Defense and Intelligence and the Committee on Foreign Policy and Inter-Parliamentary Cooperation, both chaired by members of Servant of the People.

Servant of the People has a majority on paper, but in practice it needs votes from small groups such as Dovira, For the Future, and Restoration of Ukraine, along with some opposition members, to pass legislation. Measures with cross-factional support, such as extending martial law, pass by wide margins, but on appointments and ordinary bills the ruling party repeatedly fails to muster the votes — as in January 2026, when a ministerial appointment and the ratification of an agreement were voted down in quick succession.
