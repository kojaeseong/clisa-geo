The five-caucus ruling coalition led by Civic Coalition holds 239 seats in the Sejm, above the 231 needed for a majority. It is 37 seats short, however, of the three-fifths needed to override a presidential veto (276 seats if all members are present), while Law and Justice (147 seats) and Development Plus (40 seats), which split from Law and Justice in July 2026, together hold 187 seats — more than the 185 needed to block an override if all members are present. Based on the 2023 election results, the coalition also holds a majority in the Senate, but the Senate plays no part in overriding presidential vetoes.

Who drives the agenda in the Sejm

Security legislation and consent to treaty ratification pass through the National Defence Committee (chaired by the Polish People's Party) and the Foreign Affairs Committee (chaired by Civic Coalition), and the Marshal of the Sejm, the chamber's speaker and a member of the Left, sets the floor schedule. Because the ruling coalition holds all the committee chairs and the Sejm's presiding posts, government bills are rarely blocked inside the Sejm; what actually decides the fate of government legislation is the president's signature.

The ruling coalition has the majority to pass ordinary legislation, but to override a presidential veto it needs votes from Development Plus or Law and Justice. Since December 2025, override votes have drawn only 232 to 246 votes in favor. Alliance and border measures, by contrast, win support across party lines, as with the extension of asylum restrictions at the Belarusian border (418–16 on September 4, 2026) and the ratification of the security treaty with the United Kingdom (392–3 on September 18).

The law implementing the EU's SAFE defense loans and the presidential vetoThe Russia-NATO Standoff →

Where it stands
On February 13, 2026, the Sejm passed the government's bill implementing SAFE, 236–199, with four abstentions, and on February 27 it accepted the Senate's amendments and sent the bill to the president. President Nawrocki vetoed it on March 12; on March 25 the Sejm referred the veto to committee and has held no override vote since. Using the existing Armed Forces Support Fund (FWSZ) instead, the government signed a €43.7 billion loan agreement with the EU on May 8 (Polish press reports).
The legislature's power
A law that the president has returned for reconsideration takes effect only if the Sejm passes it again by a three-fifths majority of votes cast, with at least half of its statutory members present (Article 122(5) of the Constitution). The Senate plays no part in the override.
The vote count
The five caucuses of the governing coalition hold 239 seats, 37 short of the 276 needed if all members are present. In seven override votes on other laws between December 2025 and September 2026, support ranged only from 232 to 246 votes.
Positions
The president vetoed the law, citing the long-term debt burden and the risk that the EU could withhold funds arbitrarily, and put forward as an alternative a "Polish SAFE 0%" bill that would raise the same amount from central bank profits. Defense Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz criticized it as a veto directed not at the government but at Poland's security (Polish press reports).
Next steps and deadlines
No date has been set for an override vote. The 2027 draft budget states that part of defense spending will be financed with SAFE funds through the Armed Forces Support Fund.

The 2027 budget law and defense spendingThe Russia-NATO Standoff →

Where it stands
The government submitted the 2027 draft budget to the Sejm on September 29, 2026, and the Sejm held its first reading on October 8. Defense spending totals 198.1 billion zlotys (4.51 percent of GDP), combining 131.7 billion zlotys from the state budget (3.0 percent of GDP) and 66.4 billion zlotys from the Armed Forces Support Fund.
The legislature's power
Only the government may introduce a budget bill, and the Sejm may not raise the deficit above the level in the government's draft. The president must sign the budget law within seven days and cannot veto it; he can only refer it to the Constitutional Tribunal for review (Articles 220, 221, and 224 of the Constitution).
The vote count
Like an ordinary law, it passes by a majority of those present, so the governing coalition (239 seats) can pass it on its own. The 2026 budget law passed 233–197 on December 5, 2025.
Positions
President Nawrocki signed the 2026 budget law while denouncing it as a budget of chaos, and referred it to the Constitutional Tribunal for review. The government does not recognize the Tribunal's legitimacy (Polish press reports).
Next steps and deadlines
The Sejm vote is scheduled for December 2026 (Polish broadcast reports). If the budget has not been presented to the president within four months of its submission (by January 29, 2027), the president may shorten the Sejm's term within the following 14 days (Article 225 of the Constitution).

The legal basis for military operations in the Baltic and in the airspaceThe Russia-NATO Standoff →

Where it stands
On November 21, 2025, the Sejm passed a bill to improve military operations when national security is threatened in Polish waters and to secure the Baltic Sea, 263–2, with 172 abstentions, and the president signed it on January 9, 2026.
The legislature's power
The law amends provisions on operations abroad and on forward-presence activities on allied territory (according to the Sejm's explanatory memorandum). Peacetime military operations on Polish soil are ordered by the president, at the government's request, for up to 60 days, and any extension requires the Sejm's consent. Forces engaged in such operations may destroy or disable drones that threaten people or facilities (Homeland Defense Act, Articles 11a and 25, based on the consolidated text before the amendment).
Next steps and deadlines
It could not be confirmed whether a new bill further broadening the rules of engagement has been submitted to the Sejm.
