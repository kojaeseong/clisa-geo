The president's Democratic Party holds an outright majority of 161 seats. On its own, however, it falls short of the three-fifths (180 seats) needed to fast-track bills or end a filibuster, and must win over other parties and independents. The opposition People Power Party, with 109 seats, holds more than a third of the Assembly — enough to block a constitutional amendment or the impeachment of the president.

Who drives the agenda in the National Assembly

Security legislation and the questioning of government officials fall to the National Defense Committee, the Foreign Affairs and Unification Committee, and the Intelligence Committee. The Democratic Party chairs the National Defense Committee, while the People Power Party chairs the other two, so opposition scrutiny comes mainly through questioning in those two committees.

With the Democratic Party holding an outright majority, the Assembly is poorly placed to block the president by vote, which makes opinion within the ruling party the real variable in government decisions. At the Foreign Affairs and Unification Committee on September 17, 2026, even ruling-party members called for prior consultation with the Assembly on a Hormuz deployment and said its consent would not come easily unless the safety of South Korean citizens was assured.

The timetable for transferring wartime operational controlThe Korean Peninsula →

Where it stands
The government told the National Assembly on September 10 that it aims to complete verification of full operational capability at the Security Consultative Meeting (SCM) in November. On September 16, the nominee for defense minister said that once verification is complete, the two defense ministers will set a year for the transfer and recommend it to their presidents.
The legislature's power
The transfer of wartime operational control is settled by agreement between Seoul and Washington and does not require the National Assembly's consent. The Assembly scrutinizes it through its annual parliamentary audit and through questioning.
Positions
Ruling-party members pressed for a swift transfer. A People Power Party member criticized the government, pointing to differences between Seoul and Washington and to the cancellation of counteroffensive drills in the Ulchi Freedom Shield exercise.
Next steps and deadlines
The parliamentary audit in October; the 58th SCM in November.

Aid to North Korea and a resolution condemning North Korean land minesThe Korean Peninsula →

Where it stands
The unification minister told the National Assembly that the government is preparing to send medical equipment to North Korea and has obtained a UN sanctions exemption (September 17). On September 29, the National Defense Committee sought to adopt a resolution condemning the North Korean land mine incident but failed to do so after the ruling and opposition parties could not agree on a clause calling for a review of aid to North Korea already in the works.
The legislature's power
The National Assembly can cut the budget, and inter-Korean agreements that impose a heavy fiscal burden require its consent.
The vote count
The budget passes by simple majority, so the Democratic Party (161 seats) can protect the government's proposal on its own.
Positions
The People Power Party demanded a review of aid to North Korea alongside the condemnation; the Democratic Party rejected that clause, saying the resolution should focus on condemning the North. Both parties agreed that North Korea was responsible and had violated the armistice agreement.
Next steps and deadlines
The parliamentary audit in October; review of the 2027 budget (constitutional deadline: December 2).

A deployment to the Strait of HormuzIran and Hormuz →

Where it stands
The government told the National Assembly that no decision had been made on a specific contribution (September 10). The Foreign Ministry reported that it had been reviewing options for a contribution since the crisis began and had been in contact with the United States, Britain and France (September 17).
The legislature's power
Sending South Korean troops abroad requires the National Assembly's consent (Article 60(2) of the Constitution).
The vote count
With 161 seats, the Democratic Party holds a majority on its own and could pass a consent motion without other parties. The real variable is opinion within the ruling party.
Positions
Ruling-party members called for consultation with the Assembly in advance and said consent would not come easily unless the safety of South Korean citizens was assured. A Rebuilding Korea Party member suspected that the government's visits to the United Arab Emirates and Bahrain were preparation for a deployment, and People Power Party members criticized the government for not stating a clear position.
Next steps and deadlines
Whether the government submits a consent motion for a new deployment or for changing an existing one.
