How tiles are sized
Tile area = 0.15 + 0.85 × 4 × p × (1 − p) + (size of the change in percentage points ÷ 10, up to 2), where p is the current probability. A 50% tile with no change has area 1.
4 × p × (1 − p) is the forecast’s expected Brier score (the error expected if its own probability is right), rescaled to run from 0 to 1. It is largest at 50% and treats 20% and 80% alike, so it gives the same value whether a question asks if something will or will not happen.
0.15 is the smallest possible size: a forecast at 0% or 100% with no change. Each 10 points of change adds the area of an unchanged 50% tile, up to two such areas, so large moves stand out. These numbers are judgment calls made by Clisa Geopolitics for the display.
An issue’s block area is the sum of its tiles’ areas. Blocks and tiles are both placed largest first, from the top left.
