Weekly Situation Summary

The major trends in world affairs, summarized once a week. For daily news, see Briefings.

November 23, 2026 edition

Trump leaves Asia without meeting Kim as work to expand Yongbyon's enrichment plant wraps up

President Trump headed home after the U.S.-China-Russia summit in Shenzhen on November 19 without meeting Kim Jong Un, saying the two would meet at some point. North Korea has not publicly responded to Trump's remarks about a meeting (AFP). In his opening statement to the Board of Governors on November 18, International Atomic Energy Agency Director General Rafael Grossi said construction of a further extension to the Yongbyon enrichment facility began in mid-September and was externally complete by late October, and the agency said the 5-megawatt reactor was likely continuing its seventh operating cycle. On November 17 and 18 Kim visited several sites in South Hamgyong province, including the Kim Jong Suk Naval University, and denounced naval activity by hostile forces (Korean Central News Agency). Workers' Party Secretary Jo Yong Won met President Putin in Moscow on November 16 (TASS). North Korea has still not responded, a month on, to the military talks South Korea's Defense Ministry proposed on October 23.

Iranian strikes reach a civilian plant in Bahrain as Trump says he will wait until the new year

Bahrain's Interior Ministry said on November 21 that an Iranian attack had set an industrial facility on fire, and on November 22 it said a civilian plant had been hit and two people injured, one critically. The IRGC claimed it destroyed two radars at U.S. bases in Riffa and Awali, but this has not been confirmed. Iran's army said it also targeted Kuwait's Ali Al Salem Air Base with drones on November 18, and the foreign ministries of Saudi Arabia, Qatar and Kuwait condemned what they called a dangerous escalation. In an interview with Israel's Channel 12 on November 19, President Trump said he was not rushing to make any decisions on Iran and would see what happens after the new year starts, and on November 21 he told Fox News he would make a deal that would be permanent. A Qatari delegation held two hours of talks in Tehran on the night of November 20, but Iran's deputy foreign minister, Kazem Gharibabadi, said these were not formal negotiations and that enriched uranium would not leave Iran. According to the weekly overview of the UK Maritime Trade Operations, 263 vessels transited the Strait of Hormuz in the week to November 18, down from 308 the week before, and six attacks on tankers were confirmed in the same period.

Houthis hit Riyadh's sovereign wealth fund tower as the Saudi-led coalition strikes Hodeidah port

The Saudi Press Agency said a Houthi attack early on November 19 caused material damage to part of the Public Investment Fund tower in Riyadh, and the Civil Defense said no one was hurt. The Saudi-led coalition said on November 18 that it had struck 60 targets in Sanaa, Amran, Saada and Hodeidah since November 17, and the Houthis said at least 17 people were killed. On November 20 the coalition said it struck Hodeidah port, which it said was used for smuggling and arms shipments, and the Houthis warned that if the attacks continued they would hit Saudi Red Sea ports, including Jeddah, and the Shuqaiq power plant. Since the Strait of Hormuz was closed, Saudi crude exports have been concentrated at the Red Sea port of Yanbu.

Likud and Yashar talk for four hours without a deal, two weeks before the coalition deadline

Representatives of Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar party and Prime Minister Netanyahu's Likud met for four hours in Herzliya on November 22 but did not reach a coalition agreement, and no date was set for another meeting. Eisenkot's deadline to form a government is December 6, and the president can extend it by 14 days. Israel's Channel 12 reported that Eisenkot is also weighing a minority government that would face a Knesset confidence vote. Lebanese President Joseph Aoun on November 21 demanded a full Israeli withdrawal, an end to attacks and the return of detainees.

Senate again rejects an Iran war powers resolution, 48-51, in the lame-duck session

On November 19 the U.S. Senate rejected, 48–51, a war powers concurrent resolution (H.Con.Res. 93) directing the removal of U.S. forces from hostilities with Iran (Senate roll call vote 269). Three Republicans voted yes, one Democrat voted no and one Democrat did not vote. The Senate passed a similar resolution, 50–48, on June 23 but rejected one on September 24 and has now done so again. The same day, the Pentagon's deputy inspector general told a congressional hearing that the war had cost more than $48 billion since late February. Democrats, who won majorities in both chambers in the November 3 midterm elections, will set the agenda when the new Congress convenes on January 3, 2027.

First U.S.-China-Russia summit agrees on nuclear risk talks and more oil supply, leaving out Iran and Ukraine

President Trump, President Xi Jinping and President Putin held their first three-way summit in Shenzhen on November 19, talking for about an hour and a half. A joint statement, released first by the Kremlin and then by the White House in the same wording, said a nuclear war cannot be won, committed the three to continue dialogue on nuclear risk reduction and nonproliferation, to increase oil supply and to reform the United Nations, and included a clause saying the International Criminal Court and similar bodies must not abuse their jurisdiction. It did not mention the Iran war, and Ukraine was left off the agenda. Japanese Prime Minister Takaichi and Xi only shook hands during APEC and held no bilateral meeting. The U.S.-China tariff truce and China's suspension of rare earth export controls expire on January 10, 2027.

Commerce narrows chipmaking routes for Chinese customers as "AI bubble" fears shake Asian markets

The U.S. Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security published an interim final rule in the Federal Register on November 19 that took effect immediately. The rule restricts remote access to advanced chips, formally rescinds the AI diffusion rule and adds Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil to the license requirements for advanced computing items. It also removes the ways foundries and chip packagers could rebut the presumption that advanced-node chips ordered by designers in China and other countries of concern are controlled items. The Financial Times reported that TSMC and Samsung Electronics had halted some shipments to Chinese customers. On November 23 South Korea's KOSPI fell as much as 5.8 percent in intraday trading amid fears of an AI bubble. At a news conference on November 20, President Trump said he had discussed joint AI safeguards with President Xi.

Putin rules out a deal after the Crimean Bridge strikes as the EU proposes a €45 billion loan for 2027

Ukraine struck the Crimean Bridge for a second day on the night of November 16–17, and one arch of the road section fell into the strait. Speaking in Shenzhen on November 19, President Putin said Russia would respond rather than make any agreement after the attacks on the bridge, dropped a partial ceasefire idea it had been considering and said the format of talks with Ukraine would be decided if European envoys came to Moscow. The same day, U.S. envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff attended a meeting of the coalition of the willing in Berlin hosted by Germany, France and Britain. Russia attacked Ukraine with more than 400 drones and more than 110 missiles on the night of November 19–20, killing at least eight people, and on November 22 President Zelensky urged Washington to approve the delivery of Patriot systems that allies had already paid for. The European Commission proposed a €45 billion loan for 2027 on November 18, and EU leaders will discuss it together with the next long-term budget at a special summit on November 26–27. Germany's Bundestag holds its final vote on the 2027 budget on November 27.

Russian cruise missiles land in Poland as Estonia decides to shut its road border with Russia

During a massive Russian air attack early on November 20, two cruise missiles, including a Kh-101, and what appeared to be a jet-powered drone came down in Lublin and Podkarpackie provinces in southeastern Poland. No one was hurt. NATO called it a grave violation of airspace, and Prime Minister Donald Tusk said it appeared to be a deliberate provocation. Poland did not request consultations under Article 4 of the North Atlantic Treaty but asked for more assets for NATO's Eastern Sentry mission, and NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte met Tusk in Warsaw on November 21 and pledged the alliance's solidarity. On November 20 Estonia's government decided to close all road border crossings with Russia for six months from December 1. On November 21 a logistics ship belonging to Russia's Defense Ministry was hit by a drone in international waters in the Gulf of Finland, and Finland's military said Ukraine might have been responsible.

New security alignments multiply

In August, Saudi Arabia, Turkey, and Pakistan signed the Mecca Joint Defense Agreement, and on October 5 the three countries invoked "collective deterrence measures" against Houthi attacks and decided to deploy forces rapidly to Saudi Arabia. The Turkish Grand National Assembly ratified the pact on November 5 by 279 votes to 119. North Korea, already bound to Russia by the Comprehensive Strategic Partnership Treaty it signed in 2024, has also restored relations with China following Xi Jinping's visit to Pyongyang in June. On October 30, Canada and the EU launched an "Alliance for the Future," agreeing to work toward making Canada the EU's first associate member and to expand cooperation on defense procurement, space and critical minerals. Armenian Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan said on November 6 that Armenia's interest in the Russia-led Collective Security Treaty Organization was over. Turkey and Qatar signed a mutual defense agreement at the 12th meeting of their High Strategic Committee in Istanbul on November 13.

Pakistan faces two fronts, east and west, and an emergency in its northwest

Pakistan has been fighting the Taliban government in Afghanistan since February, and after strikes on Kandahar in late September it bombed Kunar and Helmand provinces on the night of September 30. The UN Assistance Mission in Afghanistan said the strikes killed at least 10 civilians, most of them children. On the Indian front, Pakistani and Indian warships collided in the Arabian Sea on September 15. A court of arbitration has ruled on the Indus Waters Treaty, but India does not recognize the court's jurisdiction. President Zardari declared a state of emergency in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa on October 11, citing a surge in militant activity in the northwest. On October 17, Pakistani forces and the Afghan Taliban clashed at several points along the border around Kurram. Pakistan's military said it killed 74 militants over 10 days in the northwest and southwest along the Afghan border (AP, reported November 13), and on November 11 an ambush claimed by the Pakistani Taliban killed 10 police officers in Kohat, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.

Indonesia's vice president resigns, and the vote on a successor is postponed twice

Indonesian Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka told President Prabowo Subianto on November 10 that he intended to resign, and on November 12 he submitted his resignation to the leadership of the People's Consultative Assembly. Gibran had been under pressure since a court found that his educational documents did not meet the requirements for running in the election, and on November 11 all parliamentary factions in the House of Representatives filed a motion to open impeachment proceedings. On November 13 the assembly decided to elect one of two candidates nominated by the president at a plenary session on December 2, but on November 16 it moved the vote to December 9, and on November 23 Speaker Ahmad Muzani said it would be pushed back again to December 11 after critics argued that the legal time limit should be counted in working days. Gibran is the eldest son of former President Joko Widodo.

Washington puts the Monroe Doctrine into practice

In January the U.S. military captured President Maduro, and the United States then signed an oil agreement with Venezuela's transitional government. Washington has imposed an energy blockade on Cuba since January and is waging tariff wars with Canada and Brazil. In September it signed a security agreement on Greenland with Denmark. On October 14, a report said a U.S. Army battalion had begun deploying to Panama, but the U.S. Embassy in Panama denied it the same day, saying 120 soldiers of the 10th Mountain Division were taking part in a two-week jungle course at the Panamanian government's invitation. On October 21, the U.S. Embassy in Canada alleged that Canadian politicians were trying to interfere in the U.S. midterm elections. Venezuelan opposition leader Machado returned home on October 25 after more than 10 months in exile, and acting President Rodríguez said on October 29 that elections would be held but gave no date. In Brazil, President Lula won a fourth term with 51.79 percent in the October 25 runoff. On November 5 the U.S. State Department sanctioned two individuals, eight entities including two Chinese companies, and five tankers over the Cuban regime's foreign military support and illicit revenue networks; China's Commerce Ministry objected on November 7. There were also reports that Washington is negotiating a status-of-forces agreement with Venezuela's interim government that could open the way to a long-term U.S. military presence. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth visited Caracas on November 18 and said he had agreed with acting President Delcy Rodríguez to restore military ties, including joint training and intelligence sharing. Denmark's parliament ratified the security agreement with the United States and Greenland, 109–0, on November 19, and on November 20 Mexico's military captured "El Chapo Isidro," one of the FBI's ten most wanted fugitives, in an operation coordinated with U.S. intelligence agencies (Mexico's Defense Ministry).

Wars flare at once across the Horn of Africa and the Sahel

The Tigray war resumed on September 22, and on October 6 and 7 a string of witness accounts said Eritrean troops had crossed the border and entered towns in northern Tigray. In a letter dated October 8, Ethiopian Foreign Minister Gedion Timothewos called this an "all-out invasion" and cited the right of self-defense; the Eritrean government denied it and countered that Ethiopia has designs on the port of Assab. Sudan's civil war and the offensives of jihadist insurgents in the Sahel continue. On October 19, a drone attack on a market and a fuel depot in Abyei, an area contested by Sudan and South Sudan, killed at least 153 people. In September, Yemen's Houthi rebels seized the port of Mocha and Perim Island, tightening their grip on the Bab el-Mandeb Strait at the mouth of the Red Sea. Ethiopian Prime Minister Abiy said on October 28 that the country had received 12 Russian-made Su-35 fighter jets and planned to acquire the S-400 air defense system. In Sudan, an October 26 airstrike on a market in Nyala, Darfur, killed many civilians, with death tolls ranging from 21 to more than 100 depending on the group counting. In Somalia, President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud dismissed the chief justice and named a successor on November 4, and on November 5 the Supreme Court ruled that the extension of his term was unlawful and that the dismissal was beyond his authority. Ethiopia and Sudan closed each other's consulates and expelled diplomats in early November. Ethiopia's army chief of staff, Birhanu Jula, alleged on November 14 that Eritrea had led the Tigrayan rebels' offensive and that Egypt had supplied logistics and materiel; Eritrea and Egypt did not respond immediately (Anadolu Agency). In Sudan, drone attacks blamed on the Rapid Support Forces hit Khartoum and Omdurman early on November 22, killing at least four people and damaging a United Nations building.
