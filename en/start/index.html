What's coming for the world, while there's still time

Clisa Geopolitics exists to help individuals and governments make geopolitical judgments and decisions by one test: what they mean for people's lives and well-being. It puts a probability and a deadline on the major international events ahead, from wars and sanctions to elections and negotiations, shows how each would change safety, daily life and industries in different countries, and ranks the options open to governments.

How Clisa Geopolitics works

  • Primary sources. Statements by governments and international organizations, treaty texts, legislative records and pollsters' own releases are collected and verified.
  • Daily updates. Reports from the major news outlets are cross-checked each day for the Briefings, and any development that bears on a forecast can change its probability and its reasoning.
  • Scholarship put to work. International relations research, historical precedent and the ideas of thinkers from Thucydides to Mearsheimer are applied to today's issues.
  • Accountability. Every forecast names in advance the date and the source that will settle it, and results are published, right or wrong.

Connecting the events and judgments that pile up each day led Clisa Geopolitics to build two tools of its own: the Geopolitical Heat Map and Chain Reactions.

How forecasts are scored

Each forecast gets a Brier score: the squared error of its probability on each day, averaged from publication to resolution. A score of 0 is perfect; a forecaster who always says 50 percent scores 0.25. Changes made in the last seven days before resolution don't count.

Jae-Seong Ko, who runs Clisa Geopolitics from Seoul, sets its topics and editorial direction. AI (Anthropic's Claude) is used for research, drafting and translation, and he reviews the facts and judgments before anything is published.
