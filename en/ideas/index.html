Key thinkers

  • Halford Mackinder · Mackinder saw the vast plains of the Eurasian interior as a "Heartland" beyond the reach of sea power.
  • Nicholas Spykman · Inverting Mackinder, Spykman argued that the decisive zone was the "Rimland," the coastal belt surrounding the Heartland.
  • Mao Zedong · The core is the logic of protracted war: the weaker side gives up space to gain time and waits for the stronger side to wear itself out.
  • Henry Kissinger · Kissinger held that a stable order needs two pillars: a balance of power, and rules that its participants accept, that is, legitimacy.
  • Lee Kuan Yew · Lee held that China wants to be accepted as China, not as an honorary member of the West, and that it will aim to be the strongest power not just in Asia but in the world.
  • Zbigniew Brzezinski · Brzezinski held that without Ukraine, Russia cannot be a Eurasian empire.
  • Graham Allison · Allison put forward the "Thucydides Trap": of 16 cases over the past 500 years in which a rising power threatened an established hegemon, 12 ended in war.
