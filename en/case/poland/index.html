The Russia-NATO standoff: How should Poland prepare for the Russian threat?

As of October 10, 2026

The question
How should Poland prepare for the Russian threat?
Assessment
Strengthen both a permanent U.S. military presence and national resilience. In negotiating the U.S. presence, aim not at troop numbers but at real fighting capability: air defense, long-range fires, and speed of reinforcement. U.S. forces belong to another power and could be unsettled by a U.S.-Russian deal, so put Poland's own forces at the center and build up a stronger European pillar as insurance against that moment.
Most likely outcome
P1 A sustained standoff below the threshold of war 40%
Airspace violations by drones and helicopters, sabotage, GPS jamming, threats to undersea infrastructure, and nuclear threats continue, but there is no fighting between NATO and Russian forces that leaves anyone dead. The pattern of 2025–2026 persists through 2030.
Probability by outcome
P1 A sustained standoff below the threshold of war 40% · P2 A deadly incident, then a patch-up 30% · P6 Lower tensions 14.5% · P3 A Kaliningrad blockade crisis 9% · P4 Limited use of force 5% · P5 Full-scale NATO-Russia war 1.5%
The other side’s best move
Russia · Russia's most advantageous move at present is to keep imposing costs below the threshold of war while bundling, in direct talks with Washington, a ceasefire in Ukraine with limits on NATO's force posture on the eastern flank (caps on rotational forces, limits on exercises near the border). If that deal went through, Russia could sharply reduce the effect of the U.S. buildup without firing a shot. In the draft treaties it sent to the United States and NATO in December 2021, Russia likewise demanded that no forces be stationed in countries that joined NATO after May 1997. Because it is uncertain whether Washington would accept NATO's eastern-flank posture as a subject for negotiation, the feasibility of this move is rated medium; in the meantime, the standoff below the threshold of war (P1, 40% probability) continues. If the deal went through, the result would come close to lower tensions bought with a reduction in forward deployment (P6), which is better for Russia than P1. The deciding-powers analysis rates Russia's preference for P6 as Acceptable because that judgment also covers lower tensions that come without any such price. [secondary] The U.S. National Defense Strategy of January 2026, which characterized Russia as a "persistent but manageable" threat (according to a research institute's summary), widens the room for such a deal.
The likely outcome
While the war in Ukraine continues, Russia is unlikely to fight NATO directly. Instead it imposes costs through sub-threshold means such as sabotage, drones, airspace violations, and nuclear threats, and drives wedges between Poland's president and its government and between the United States and Europe. The two sides' best moves therefore converge on a sustained standoff below the threshold of war (40%) and on an incident that costs lives but is patched up (30%).
What it means for people
The risk to Poles is concentrated in a full-scale NATO-Russia war, put at a 1.5 percent probability. In that event, their personal-safety score would fall from 91.5 to 30.5, and that of Baltic citizens from 82.5 to 17.3.

Key assessments

A permanent U.S. military presence is aimed at a large-scale ground attack, but the sabotage, drones, and airspace violations Poland is actually experiencing cannot be stopped by a tripwire.

ReasoningAs Schelling showed with the case of the Berlin Wall, where a commitment leaves room for interpretation, the adversary expects the defender to avoid a forceful response. Russia's choice of sub-threshold means exploits this structure. What Poland should seek in negotiations over the U.S. presence, therefore, is less troop numbers than the capability to actually fight—air defense, long-range fires, and speed of reinforcement—and against sub-threshold attacks it should combine resilience with selective punishment (public attribution, sanctions, and proportionate responses at the alliance level).
