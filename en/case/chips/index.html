Clisa Geopolitics · Strategic Analysis

The AI and Chips issue has been removed from the issues under analysis

Since October 2026, Clisa Geopolitics has measured what an issue means for people across the UN's seven categories of human security (personal safety, community, political, and economic, plus alerts for food, health, and environment), and it treats as issues for analysis those in which people's lives are heavily at stake in these categories. By this standard, AI and Chips does not stand as an issue in its own right, so it has been removed from the issues under analysis.

The U.S.-China chip rivalry remains on the map as the flashpoint "U.S.-China chip rivalry and Korean fabs in China" and is treated as an external variable in other issues, such as the Taiwan Strait.

Forecasts already published, f46–f53, will not be deleted; they will remain in the Forecasts and Track Record tab until their resolution dates, when their outcomes will be settled.

Forecasts still open

  • f46 · By January 31, 2027, a company or government announcement, or a report by Reuters, Bloomberg, or Yonhap, confirms that the United States has issued, for each of Samsung's Xi'an plant and SK hynix's Wuxi and Dalian plants, an equipment import license valid for at least six months of 2027 (annual licenses, multiyear licenses, and restoration of VEU status all count)
  • f47 · By December 31, 2026, the U.S. president signs a Section 232 proclamation that directly includes memory integrated circuits (HTS 8542.32) among the products subject to tariffs (resolves yes if they are among the covered products even with investment-linked exemptions or quotas attached; resolves no if only finished products containing memory are covered)
  • f48 · By June 30, 2027, Samsung Electronics or SK hynix announces a decision to build a memory wafer fab in the United States (excluding packaging and foundry) in a regulatory filing or press release that specifies the site or the investment amount (conditional reviews, memorandums of understanding, and reports that a plan is under consideration do not count)
  • f49 · By June 30, 2027, China's Ministry of Commerce newly places a South Korean company, or a subsidiary at least 50 percent owned by a South Korean company, on the export control list, the Unreliable Entity List, the countermeasure list under the Anti-Foreign Sanctions Law, or a list under State Council Decree No. 835; or the suspension of sanctions on Hanwha Ocean's subsidiaries is not extended and the sanctions take effect again (excluded if the lapse is merely a technical gap and the suspension is renewed within 30 days)
  • f50 · As of January 15, 2027, Ministry of Commerce Announcements Nos. 55–58, 61, and 62 of 2025 (the October 2025 expansion of rare-earth export controls) remain suspended or have been withdrawn (as confirmed by a Ministry of Commerce announcement or a joint U.S.-China announcement)
  • f51 · By March 31, 2027, volume-production shipments of HBM3 or HBM3E by CXMT (excluding samples, risk production, and units for qualification testing) are confirmed by an official CXMT announcement or by reports from at least two of Reuters, Bloomberg, TrendForce, and DigiTimes
  • f52 · In any quarter of 2027, CXMT's share of global DRAM revenue is reported at 15 percent or higher (by Counterpoint or TrendForce)
  • f53 · By January 3, 2027 (the end of the 119th Congress), a law (a stand-alone bill or the National Defense Authorization Act) is enacted with the president's signature that contains at least one of the following: a provision banning sales of equipment and services to designated companies such as CXMT and YMTC, or a provision mandating U.S. extraterritorial controls if allies do not align by a deadline
