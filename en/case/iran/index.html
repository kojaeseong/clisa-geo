The Iran war and Hormuz: what South Korea should protect and what it should give up

As of October 5, 2026

The question
Amid the Iran war and the blockade of Hormuz, what should South Korea protect and what should it give up?
Assessment
Build around an energy transition—diversifying sources of crude imports and expanding stockpiles—while keeping a separate diplomatic channel to Iran, and pursue an expanded mission for the Cheonghae Unit only if its area of operations is confined to waters outside the Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf. Freezing or disposing of Iranian assets in South Korea and cooperating in enforcing the U.S. blockade and sanctions are the most direct paths to Iranian retaliation.
Most likely outcome
I1 A prolonged, escalated war 32%
With no agreement, the U.S. blockade, Iranian attacks in the strait, and proxy attacks on the routes that bypass the strait all continue. Much of the crude oil still flows, carried by shuttle tankers under U.S. military escort and along bypass routes, but incidents keep recurring and petroleum products and LNG are in short supply. This is the future in which the conditions of September and October 2026 persist.
Probability by outcome
I1 A prolonged, escalated war 32% · I6 Incomplete reopening 24% · I2 End of the war and full reopening 16% · I3 Regime collapse or civil war in Iran 12% · I4 Collapse of Gulf exports 11% · I5 A nuclear-armed Iran 5%
The other side’s best move
Iran · The best course is to widen passage for non-adversarial countries, prying the United States away from its allies, while pursuing a phased agreement that trades the strait's reopening for the lifting of the blockade and sanctions step by step and defers the nuclear question. The strait is greater leverage when opened selectively than when closed.
The likely outcome
Both the United States and Iran want the strait reopened, but a deal trading the strait's reopening for the lifting of the blockade and sanctions in phases was signed once, in June 2026, and collapsed within three weeks. Since then Washington, believing it is winning, has rejected a phased roadmap.
What it means for people
The harm of this war falls most heavily on Iranians. In the future in which the Iranian regime collapses (12%), about 100 people per 100,000 would die each year and 10 percent of the population would be driven from their homes. For South Koreans, the risk in this issue is to income: if Gulf exports collapse again (11%), incomes would fall by about 9 percent.

ReasoningThe Hankuk Chemi, seized in January 2021, was released only after Seoul agreed to unfreeze $1 billion of the Iranian funds frozen in South Korea. On top of the risks every ship faces in wartime, South Korea bears additional risk from two sources: measures to freeze or dispose of Iranian assets in South Korea, and the presence of South Korean warships inside the Strait of Hormuz. On September 5, 2026, Iran warned through a senior official that it would regard any South Korean involvement in the strait as joining the aggression, and on September 7 its Foreign Ministry spokesperson issued a warning to the same effect, in Korean, to any country stationing forces in the Persian Gulf and the strait.
