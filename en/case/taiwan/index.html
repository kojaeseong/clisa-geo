The Taiwan Strait: What should South Korea prepare for, and what should it promise, in a Taiwan contingency?

As of October 5, 2026

The question
What should South Korea prepare for a Taiwan Strait crisis, and how far should it commit?
Assessment
South Korea should prepare, confidentially, procedures for rear-area support under which it accepts the dispatch of USFK to Taiwan only on two conditions — prior consultation between Seoul and Washington, and measures to fill any resulting gap in deterrence on the Korean Peninsula — and put in place economic and civilian preparedness, including substitutes for and stockpiles of goods it relies on China for, such as rare earths. A crisis communication channel with China should be institutionalized in peacetime, without expecting it to work during a crisis. An explicit commitment to contribute to the alliance, formalizing South Korean military participation in a Taiwan contingency, ranks lowest.
Most likely outcome
T1 Tense status quo 50%
Large-scale exercises recur once or twice a year and gray-zone pressure continues, but it does not escalate to inspections or a blockade. Political divisions in Taiwan and Washington's transactional bent push tensions up and down.
Probability by outcome
T1 Tense status quo 50% · T5 Brokered stability 20% · T2 Gray-zone escalation 18% · T3 Blockade or quarantine 7% · T4 Armed conflict 5%
The other side’s best move
China · The best course is to keep pressure routine while avoiding a turn to force until the 2028 election, to win concessions from the U.S. administration on the Taiwan language in joint documents and on arms sales, and to work through Taiwan's opposition in parallel. With the military high command hollowed out, using force now would be a gamble.
The likely outcome
Because Taiwan is concentrating on deterrence and China is biding its time, the two strategies meet in a tense status quo likely to last until Taiwan's presidential election in January 2028. What comes after depends on what deal the U.S. administration makes with China, how far Congress slows that deal, and how Taiwanese voters, who have pushed back against outside pressure in the past, choose.
What it means for people
Armed conflict would gravely threaten the lives and safety of people in Taiwan. The first thing South Korea should carry out is its plan to evacuate and protect South Koreans in Taiwan and South Korean seafarers transiting the strait.

Key assessments

The course of events in the Taiwan Strait depends heavily on the deal the U.S. administration strikes with China, but the administration cannot set the terms of that deal on its own.

ReasoningWhen the Carter administration broke diplomatic relations with Taiwan in 1979, Congress enacted the Taiwan Relations Act, offsetting much of the effect. Because the power to initiate arms sales rests with the executive branch, however, Congress can slow the administration's concessions to China but would find it hard to reverse them.
