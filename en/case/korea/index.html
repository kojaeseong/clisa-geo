The Korean Peninsula: What should South Korea do about North Korea?

As of October 5, 2026

The question
Amid North Korea's nuclear program and great-power deal-making, what should South Korea do?
Assessment
Seoul should strengthen conventional deterrence and, as insurance, build a latent nuclear capability — enrichment and reprocessing within the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty — while tying its pace to whether North Korea freezes its program. In any negotiation over a freeze, Seoul should secure a seat at the table for the implementation and peace-regime stages that would follow a U.S.-North Korean agreement. The cards it plays early should be steps that lower tensions, such as adjusting joint U.S.-South Korean exercises or restoring inter-Korean liaison channels.
Most likely outcome
F1 Armed stalemate 37%
A hostile two-state relationship hardens. North Korea expands its nuclear weapons and missiles and builds nuclear-powered submarines with Russian help. Washington wants dialogue, but North Korea refuses on the grounds that denuclearization is on the agenda, or the two sides meet without reaching agreement. There is no major clash, but there are no communication lines and no reunions either. The number of surviving separated-family members falls to about 20,000 by 2030.
Probability by outcome
F1 Armed stalemate 37% · F5 A deal without South Korea 22% · F3 Nuclear dominoes 19% · F2 Managed coexistence 12% · F4 Sudden upheaval 10%
The other side’s best move
North Korea · A direct deal with Washington that leaves South Korea out is the best move. The core of the exchange is de facto acceptance of its nuclear status and a scaling back of joint exercises and U.S. strategic assets; sanctions relief is a side item (the formula entrenched since Kim Yo Jong's statement of July 2020).
The likely outcome
North Korea's best move and the Trump administration's preferences both point toward a direct deal between Washington and Pyongyang that leaves Seoul out. What Pyongyang wants most in return is de facto acceptance of its nuclear status and a scaling back of joint U.S.-South Korean exercises — and since February 2026, it has said it will not deal with the South.
What it means for people
Even if these recommendations are carried out, South Koreans' life-and-safety score on the personal well-being index is likely to fall below its 2026 level. The largest gain would come in personal relationships, as separated families meet again and exchange news.

Key assessments

For South Korea, a risk as real as war is being shut out of the agreements that deal with North Korea.

ReasoningSouth Korea was not a party to the negotiations over the 1994 Agreed Framework, yet it paid 70 percent of the cost of building the light-water reactors, and right after the 2018 Singapore summit the United States announced a halt to joint U.S.-South Korean exercises without consulting Seoul beforehand.

What North Korea wants most in return for negotiating is not sanctions relief but de facto U.S. acceptance of its nuclear status, and what South Korea can influence lies at the edges of that: adjustments to joint U.S.-South Korean exercises and to deployments of U.S. strategic assets.

ReasoningSince Kim Yo Jong's statement of July 2020, North Korea has given priority, over sanctions relief, to the withdrawal of the U.S. "hostile policy," including a halt to joint exercises and to strategic-asset deployments. Even here, however, what South Korea holds is limited influence, not a veto.

South Korea's troop shortage around 2040 cannot be closed by lengthening military service, and it coincides with a period when South Korea's share of the burden within the alliance grows.

ReasoningUnder the current system, South Korea's armed forces would shrink to about 270,000 by 2040, widening the troop ratio with North Korea from 1:2.8 to 1:4.7, and keeping 250,000 enlisted soldiers would require extending compulsory service to 31–33 months. The 2026 U.S. National Defense Strategy states that South Korea is capable of taking primary responsibility for deterring North Korea.
