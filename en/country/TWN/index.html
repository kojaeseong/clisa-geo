Taiwan

President Lai Ching-te (Democratic Progressive Party); Vice President Hsiao Bi-khim

The Democratic Progressive Party (DPP) government wants to raise defense spending to more than 3 percent of GDP in 2027 (NT$1.12 trillion), but the opposition-controlled Legislative Yuan, dominated by the Kuomintang (KMT) and the Taiwan People's Party (TPP), keeps checking it, cutting a special defense budget in May from $40 billion to $25 billion. Confidence in American security guarantees is wavering as the Trump administration floats arms sales to Taiwan as a bargaining chip with Beijing and speaks of not supporting Taiwanese independence. No large-scale, code-named PLA encirclement exercise has been confirmed in 2026, but gray-zone pressure, such as Chinese coast guard incursions around Kinmen, has become routine.

Grand strategy

De facto independence (the status quo) and the survival of its democracy.

Must achieve

  • Keeping the U.S. security commitment
  • Higher defense spending and asymmetric capabilities
  • The "silicon shield" of its semiconductor supply chain

Can concede

  • Refraining from explicit steps such as a declaration of independence
  • Semiconductor investment in the United States (local production)

Will never concede

  • Accepting "one country, two systems"
  • Negotiating under the threat of force
  • Its democracy

The Lai government wants to raise defense spending above 3 percent of GDP, but the opposition-controlled legislature keeps cutting the budget. Trust in Washington is wavering as the United States floats arms sales to Taiwan as a bargaining chip with Beijing.

Recent developments

  • September 28, 2026U.S. Ambassador to China David Perdue said Trump had asked Xi at the Washington summit whether China would be willing to buy American weapons, and that the $14 billion Taiwan arms package, on hold since the May summit in Beijing, still has no approval date.

  • September 25, 2026After the U.S.-China summit, Washington reaffirmed that its Taiwan policy had not changed (the Taiwan Relations Act and the Six Assurances), but Trump avoided commenting publicly.
  • August 27, 2026The Legislative Yuan passed a supplementary budget of NT$240 billion for drones and unmanned systems.
  • August 20, 2026The Executive Yuan approved a 2027 defense budget proposal of $35.1 billion, up 18 percent from the previous year.
  • August 2026Taiwan held its largest-ever Han Kuang exercise, with about 20,000 reservists and 10,000 civil servants taking part, including drills for degraded communications.
  • May 8, 2026The Legislative Yuan passed the special defense budget after cutting it from $40 billion to $25 billion, dropping domestic drone and air-defense programs.

What to watch

  • Whether U.S. arms sales to Taiwan resume, and whether a U.S.-China "grand bargain" includes language on Taiwan
  • The results of the November 28 local elections and the spread of the KMT's China-friendly line under party chair Cheng Li-wun
  • Whether large-scale, code-named PLA exercises resume, and gray-zone pressure around Kinmen and Matsu
