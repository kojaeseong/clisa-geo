Ukraine

President Volodymyr Zelensky (Prime Minister Serhii Koretskyi, appointed July 16, 2026)

Power Structures · Volodymyr ZelenskyArmed forces · Ukraine

Ukraine is fighting a defensive war in eastern Donetsk while using drones and missiles to strike Russia's rear, including Novorossiysk, and its shadow fleet. Talks stalled as Washington's attention shifted to the Iran war; a September visit by U.S. envoys has put a resumption of trilateral talks back on the table. A major cabinet reshuffle in July reset Kyiv's wartime economic and diplomatic course.

Grand strategy

Survival and sovereignty as an independent state.

Must achieve

  • Binding security guarantees that deter another invasion
  • Integration with the West (EU membership first)
  • Funding for postwar reconstruction

Can concede

  • Accepting the reality of de facto Russian control of occupied territory (not legal recognition)
  • The timing of NATO membership
  • Some limits on the size of its armed forces

Will never concede

  • Legal cession of occupied territory
  • A security arrangement in which Russia holds a veto
  • Demilitarization

Ukraine has shifted its priority from recovering territory to national survival and preventing another invasion. Its current course is to raise the cost of the war by hitting Russia's rear—oil refineries, the Black Sea Fleet and the shadow fleet—while moving in step with Europe to reject unfavorable terms in U.S.-led negotiations.
