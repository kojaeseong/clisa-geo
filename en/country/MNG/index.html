Central and Inner Asia

Mongolia

President Ukhnaagiin Khurelsukh, Prime Minister Nyam-Osor Uchral (took office March 30, 2026)

Mongolia has gone through political turmoil, with three changes of prime minister in the year after June 2025, and after Prime Minister Zandanshatar resigned in March 2026, Uchral took office promising mining reform. While keeping up its "third neighbor" diplomacy between China and Russia, Mongolia is widening its economic ties with Russia, gaining SCO partner status in September and bringing an interim trade agreement with the EAEU into force in July. Its dependence on Russian fuel and the deadlock in price talks over the Power of Siberia 2 gas pipeline are the main variables.

Grand strategy

Independence and democracy between two giants.

Must achieve

  • "Third neighbor" diplomacy (the United States, Japan, South Korea, the EU)
  • Mining export revenue
  • Neutrality

Can concede

  • Dependence on Russian fuel
  • Mineral exports to China

Will never concede

  • Entry of either neighbor's troops
  • Sovereignty

Even amid political turmoil that has brought three prime ministers in a year, Mongolia is trying to secure room to maneuver through its "third neighbor" diplomacy. Yet SCO partner status and the EAEU trade agreement are widening its economic ties with Russia.

Recent developments

  • September 3, 2026At the Eastern Economic Forum in Vladivostok, Prime Minister Uchral discussed a stable fuel supply with Russia's energy minister.
  • September 1, 2026President Khurelsukh met President Putin at the SCO summit in Bishkek, and Mongolia obtained SCO partner status.
  • July 2026An interim trade agreement between the Eurasian Economic Union (EAEU) and Mongolia entered into force.

What to watch

  • China-Russia price talks on the Power of Siberia 2 pipeline and whether a route through Mongolia is finalized
  • Coalition stability and the shape of the 2027 presidential race
  • Fuel supplies amid Russian restrictions on exports of petroleum products
