South Korea

President Lee Jae-myung (Democratic Party of Korea); Prime Minister Han Seong-sook (in office since July 2026; former Naver CEO)

Power Structures · Lee Jae-myungArmed forces · South Korea

Under the banner of "peaceful coexistence," the Lee Jae-myung government set out three principles at the UN General Assembly in September and is urging Washington to ease sanctions on North Korea and hold a U.S.-North Korea summit. Relations with the United States, by contrast, are strained by Seoul's condition that projects under its $350 billion investment pledge be commercially reasonable and by its refusal of a U.S. request to send forces to Hormuz, and alliance trust was shaken when Trump ordered the Ulchi Freedom Shield exercise scaled back without prior notice. With China, relations have continued to improve since the January summit in Beijing, including the partial removal of structures in the Yellow Sea.

Grand strategy

To prevent war on the Korean Peninsula and safeguard prosperity.

Must achieve

  • Managing tensions and achieving peaceful coexistence with North Korea
  • Maintaining the U.S.-South Korea alliance and defending economic interests in the United States (tariffs and investment)
  • Stable relations with China
  • Technological competitiveness in semiconductors, shipbuilding, and other industries

Can concede

  • The size and form of investment in the United States
  • The scale of combined exercises
  • The approach to sanctions on North Korea (a freeze first, instead of denuclearization)

Will never concede

  • Decisions on Korean Peninsula issues made without South Korea
  • Overseas deployments that would draw it into war (Hormuz)
  • Encroachment on sovereign areas, including the Yellow Sea

Putting "peaceful coexistence" first, the Lee Jae-myung government is urging U.S.-North Korea dialogue and has floated trading sanctions relief for a nuclear freeze. At the same time, it is drawing lines against U.S. demands for troops and investment. Its keynote is a pragmatic course that leans too far toward neither Washington nor Beijing.

Recent developments

  • October 5, 2026The Joint Chiefs of Staff concluded that the September 21 explosion in the Demilitarized Zone (two soldiers seriously wounded, one lightly wounded) was caused by a resin-cased antipersonnel mine planted by North Korean troops about 10 meters south of the Military Demarcation Line, and the UN Command assessed it as deliberately planted within the previous 12 months. The military detonated and removed the mine the same day.
  • September 27, 2026The presidential office called President Zelensky's disclosure at the UN General Assembly on September 24 of the transfer of two North Korean prisoners of war to South Korea a breach of a confidential agreement and demanded an explanation and an apology from Ukraine; on September 28, it expressed strong regret after the Ukrainian side denied that any such agreement existed.
  • September 27, 2026The Joint Chiefs of Staff and the UN Command jointly investigated a suspected mine explosion in the Demilitarized Zone near Paju on September 21 that left officers seriously wounded, but the risk of uncleared mines kept investigators from reaching the actual blast site, and the cause has not yet been determined.
  • September 22, 2026In his address to the UN General Assembly, President Lee laid out three principles of "peaceful coexistence" with North Korea and proposed that Washington secure a nuclear freeze in exchange for easing sanctions.
  • September 18, 2026President Lee set out the principle that South Korea will not send troops to take part in the war over Hormuz. The government is considering only an expanded role for the Cheonghae Unit.
  • August 17, 2026On President Trump's orders, the U.S.-South Korea Ulchi Freedom Shield (UFS) exercise was shortened (August 17–21) and reduced in scale, and the South Korean government received no advance notice.
  • August 17, 2026Former Prime Minister Kim Min-seok defeated Jung Chung-rae to become leader of the Democratic Party. Kim stepped down as prime minister on June 30 and was succeeded by Han Seong-sook.
  • June 3, 2026In the local elections, the Democratic Party won 12 metropolitan mayoralties and provincial governorships and the People Power Party won four; in Seoul, Oh Se-hoon won reelection as mayor.

What to watch

  • Friction with Washington over investment in the United States and contributions in Hormuz, and whether U.S. Forces Korea or combined exercises are cut further
  • The risk that South Korea is bypassed if a U.S.-North Korea summit takes place
  • Continued severance of inter-Korean ties under North Korea's "two hostile states" doctrine
