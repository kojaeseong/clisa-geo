East Asia

China

Xi Jinping (general secretary of the Chinese Communist Party, president and chairman of the Central Military Commission); Premier Li Qiang

Power Structures · Xi JinpingArmed forces · China

Since the October 2025 truce in Busan, Washington and Beijing have kept up summit diplomacy—Trump's visit to China in May, Xi's visit to the United States on September 23–25—and built a framework of "strategic stability"; the tariff truce has been extended by two months, to January 2027. Beijing has pocketed diplomatic gains, including a U.S. statement that it does not support Taiwan independence, while keeping up pressure on Japan for an 11th month since Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi's remarks on Taiwan: a ban on dual-use exports, curbs on seafood and restrictions on tourism. At home, the purge of Zhang Youxia and Liu Zhenli has shrunk the Central Military Commission to the point of near dissolution, and growth slowed to 4.3 percent in the second quarter.

Grand strategy

The perpetuation of Communist Party rule, and the "great rejuvenation of the Chinese nation"—matching or surpassing the United States—by the middle of the century.

Must achieve

  • Unification with Taiwan (creating the conditions comes before any deadline)
  • Technological self-reliance in chips and AI (the core of the 15th Five-Year Plan)
  • Completing the capability to deny U.S. forces access inside the first island chain
  • Loosening the U.S. alliance network

Can concede

  • Part of the trade imbalance (purchase commitments)
  • The pace and intensity of export controls on rare earths and other goods
  • The level of support for North Korea and Russia
  • Temporary stabilization of individual territorial disputes, such as the border with India

Will never concede

  • Taiwan independence, or a formal foreign military commitment to defend Taiwan
  • The Party's political monopoly, and Xinjiang, Tibet and Hong Kong
  • Formally abandoning its South China Sea claims

China's strategy is to avoid a head-on clash with the United States while making time work in its favor. Summit diplomacy that stages "strategic stability" slows tariff and technology pressure, and Beijing uses the breathing room to push technological self-reliance and military modernization. With its neighbors, it calibrates economic retaliation (Japan) and gray-zone pressure (Taiwan, the Philippines) without crossing the threshold of all-out war.

  • May 15, 2026On May 13–15, President Trump made a state visit to China, where the two sides agreed on the purchase of 200 Boeing airliners and on allowing exports of Nvidia's H200 chips, and defined their relationship as one of "constructive strategic stability."

What to watch

  • Whether a grand bargain is struck before the tariff truce expires in January 2027, and whether chip export controls are eased
  • How the purge of the military leadership affects readiness for Taiwan operations and appointments at the 2027 Party Congress
  • Whether economic retaliation against Japan (rare earths, seafood) continues or is lifted
