Oceania

Australia

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese (Labor)

In its April 2026 National Defence Strategy, Australia committed to raising defense spending to around 3 percent of GDP by 2033, and it is accelerating expanded U.S. rotational deployments and the AUKUS nuclear-submarine program. In July its alliance treaty with Papua New Guinea (the Pukpuk Treaty) entered into force, giving Australia its first new alliance in more than 70 years, since ANZUS, and extending its security cooperation across the Pacific. In September, nuclear-capable B-52s deployed to RAAF Base Tindal. While naming China as its principal threat, Canberra is diversifying its partnerships with Vietnam, India and others.

Grand strategy

Preventing a China-dominated order in the Indo-Pacific, with the U.S. alliance as the axis.

Must achieve

  • AUKUS nuclear-powered submarines
  • Defense spending at around 3 percent of GDP by 2033
  • A security network among Pacific island nations (the alliance treaty with PNG)

Can concede

  • Normalizing trade with China
  • Part of its defense procurement

Will never concede

  • The U.S. alliance
  • Chinese military bases in Pacific island nations

Australia has named China as its principal threat and fortified its southern line of defense by expanding U.S. rotational deployments, accelerating AUKUS and bringing the alliance treaty with PNG into force.

Recent developments

  • September 23, 2026At the UN General Assembly, Albanese formally launched Australia's bid for a nonpermanent seat on the Security Council for 2029–30.
  • September 3, 2026At a meeting between Defence Minister Richard Marles and U.S. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, Washington announced that it would accelerate the expansion of U.S. forces in Australia.
  • September 1, 2026The first U.S. B-52 bombers arrived at the upgraded RAAF Base Tindal in the Northern Territory.
  • July 8, 2026The mutual defense treaty with Papua New Guinea (the Pukpuk Treaty) entered into force.

What to watch

  • Domestic opinion on the AUKUS submarine timeline and expanded U.S. forward deployments
  • Balancing trade with China against security
  • Competition with China for influence among Pacific island nations (PNG, the Solomon Islands and others)
