Southeast Asia

Indonesia

President Prabowo Subianto

Under the banner of its "free and active" foreign policy, Indonesia continues to hedge between the United States and China. In August 2026 it held a joint sailing exercise with the Chinese navy in waters east of Taiwan while also hosting a senior Pentagon official. At home, student protests over fuel prices, a weak rupiah and the free-meals program flared again, and in September, amid the threat of a downgrade from emerging to frontier status in MSCI indexes and doubts about fiscal credibility, Prabowo abruptly fired his finance minister.

Grand strategy

A nonaligned, independent ("free and active") foreign policy and domestic development.

Must achieve

  • High growth and industrialization through resource processing
  • Preserving the bargaining power that comes from neutrality amid great-power rivalry
  • Sovereignty over the Natuna waters

Can concede

  • Military exchanges with both the United States and China
  • Terms of external economic engagement

Will never concede

  • Joining either side's alliance
  • Sovereignty over the Natuna exclusive economic zone

The Prabowo government hedges by, for example, holding a joint sailing exercise with the Chinese navy and hosting a senior Pentagon official in the same month. As confidence in its public finances wavers, its room to maneuver abroad is narrowing too.

Recent developments

  • September 14, 2026Prabowo dismissed Finance Minister Purbaya Yudhi Sadewa and appointed Deputy Minister Suahasil Nazara to replace him.
  • August 2026A joint sailing exercise with Chinese naval vessels in waters east of Taiwan raised concern in the region.
  • June 2026Student protests against fuel price hikes, the weak rupiah and a poorly run free-meals program spread to Jakarta and other cities.
  • June 2026MSCI kept Indonesia in its emerging-markets index but maintained its warning of a downgrade to frontier status.

What to watch

  • Fiscal discipline under the new finance minister, and stability in the rupiah and capital markets
  • How expanding military cooperation with China affects Jakarta's position on sovereignty in the Natuna Sea
  • Whether protests over the economy spread again
