Saudi Arabia

King Salman bin Abdulaziz (Crown Prince and Prime Minister Mohammed bin Salman)

Since the opening days of the Iran war, Saudi Arabia has come under Iranian missile and drone attacks that damaged facilities including the Ras Tanura refinery, and since July, Houthi offensives and drone attacks from Iraq have piled on. Even the East-West Pipeline, the alternative export route around the Hormuz closure, was halted by an attack on September 11 and restarted at a low level on the 22nd, leaving export capacity severely constrained. Riyadh has moved to diversify its security by signing a joint defense pact with Turkey and Pakistan.

Grand strategy

Stability of the monarchy and the economic transformation of Vision 2030.

Must achieve

  • Secure oil export routes (bypassing Hormuz)
  • U.S. security guarantees and civil nuclear cooperation
  • Checking Iran's regional hegemony

Can concede

  • OPEC+ production adjustments
  • The terms of normalization with Israel (the question of a Palestinian state)
  • The level of its relations with China

Will never concede

  • The legitimacy of the monarchy
  • The security of the holy cities of Mecca and Medina
  • A nuclear-armed Iran

When the Iran war brought direct attacks on its oil infrastructure, Saudi Arabia acted on the conclusion that the United States alone was not enough. Its joint defense pact with nuclear-armed Pakistan and NATO member Turkey is the proof.

  • When the East-West Pipeline returns to full operation, and crude export volumes
  • Houthi offensives along Yemen's Red Sea coast and the intensity of the Saudi response
  • Whether civil nuclear cooperation with the United States is tied to normalization with Israel
