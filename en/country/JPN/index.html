East Asia

Japan

Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi (president of the Liberal Democratic Party); Emperor Naruhito

In the February 8, 2026, general election, Prime Minister Takaichi led the LDP to 316 seats on its own (352 with its coalition partner), the largest landslide of the postwar era, giving her a strong political base. Since her November 2025 remark that a Taiwan contingency could constitute a "survival-threatening situation" for Japan, China has kept up pressure for 11 months—banning dual-use exports, halting seafood imports and restricting tourism—but she has not retracted it. Tokyo is responding by reaching the target of defense spending at 2 percent of GDP ahead of schedule, revising its three security documents and cooperating with Australia on missile testing. On September 22, just before the U.S.–China summit, she met Trump and reaffirmed the alliance and the tariff agreement.

Grand strategy

Preventing China from changing the status quo, with the U.S.–Japan alliance as the axis.

Must achieve

  • A fundamental buildup of defense capabilities (early revision of the three security documents, counterstrike capability, long-term defense spending increases)
  • Stability in the Taiwan Strait (seen as bound up with Japan's own survival)
  • A wider security network with Australia, the Philippines and India

Can concede

  • The cost to economic ties with China (absorbing export bans and seafood retaliation)
  • Trade terms with the United States
  • Alliance cost-sharing

Will never concede

  • Sovereignty over the Senkakus
  • A hollowing-out of the U.S.–Japan alliance
  • A retreat to the position that a Taiwan contingency has nothing to do with Japan's security

Riding its February election landslide, the Takaichi government is moving quickly to normalize Japan's defense posture. It has not retracted the Taiwan remark despite 11 months of Chinese economic retaliation, and it turns Washington's push to shift defense burdens onto allies into a rationale for self-strengthening.

Recent developments

  • September 22, 2026Takaichi held a summit with Trump in New York and reaffirmed cooperation on the tariff agreement, the Quad, the Free and Open Indo-Pacific (FOIP) and the abduction issue.
  • September 17, 2026In the reshuffle that formed her second cabinet, Takaichi kept Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi and Defense Minister Koizumi in their posts, signaling continuity.
  • September 11, 2026Defense Minister Shinjiro Koizumi said he does not rule out acquiring nuclear-powered submarines.
  • August 18, 2026The Japanese and Australian defense ministers agreed to test Japanese-made hypersonic and long-range missiles in Australia.
  • July 7, 2026Chinese and Japanese coast guard vessels faced off in Senkaku territorial waters over a Japanese fishing boat, the second such standoff after one on June 10.

What to watch

  • Whether China sustains or expands economic retaliation against Japan, and whether leader-level dialogue resumes
  • The credibility of U.S. security commitments to Taiwan and Japan amid U.S.–China détente
  • Revision of the three security documents and debate over further defense increases (including nuclear-powered submarines)
