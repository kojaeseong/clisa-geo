Power Structures · Donald Trump

Through reciprocal state visits with China, to Beijing in May and Washington in September, the Trump administration is pursuing a tariff truce (through January 2027) and "strategic stability," and it has floated arms sales to Taiwan as a bargaining chip, unsettling allies. After the Supreme Court ruled the IEEPA tariffs unlawful in February, Washington moved to Section 122 (10 percent) and then, from July 24, imposed Section 301 "forced labor" tariffs on 60 countries (12.5 percent on South Korea and Japan, 10 percent on Taiwan and the EU, and an additional 12.5 percent on China). With the Iran war that began in late February and the Hormuz crisis still unresolved, it is pressing South Korea to send troops, has unilaterally scaled back the combined U.S.-South Korea exercises, and is seeking a summit with Kim Jong Un before year's end.

Grand strategy

To protect the homeland and preserve dominance in the Western Hemisphere while ensuring that no country surpasses the United States. That country is China.

Must achieve

  • Keep China from overtaking the United States in advanced semiconductors and AI
  • Lighten the U.S. burden by handing allies primary responsibility for their own defense
  • Bring manufacturing and energy supply chains back home

Can concede

  • Primary responsibility for allied defense, such as Europe's defense against Russia and South Korea's conventional deterrence of North Korea
  • Rhetorical formulations on Taiwan (such as not supporting independence)
  • Part of Ukraine's territory in a settlement of the Russia-Ukraine war
  • Damage to alliance relationships in the course of trade negotiations

Will never concede

  • Transfer of core technologies to China
  • The dollar's reserve-currency status and command of the oceans
  • Leaving unchecked nuclear and missile capabilities aimed at the homeland (Iran's nuclear program, North Korea's ICBMs)

The 2026 National Defense Strategy ranks homeland defense first, deterring China second, and allied burden sharing third, and it enshrines U.S. dominance in the Western Hemisphere as the "Trump Corollary" to the Monroe Doctrine. Rather than defending every front in Eurasia directly, the United States is handing primary responsibility to regional allies and shifting toward the role of an offshore balancer that holds the balance with decisive capabilities in nuclear weapons, sea power, and technology. Where it defines a threat as one to the homeland, however, it uses force directly, as in the Iran war.
