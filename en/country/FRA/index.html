Europe

France

President Emmanuel Macron / Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu (reappointed October 10, 2025)

In a parliament with no majority, Prime Minister Lecornu is hanging on, surviving one no-confidence threat after another. Ahead of the 2027 presidential election, the National Rally (RN) leads in the polls. In March, Macron announced an increase in nuclear warheads and a plan for extended deterrence across Europe, and France agreed to lead a Hormuz maritime mission with Britain, though the mission has stalled. Having missed its 2026 deficit target, the government has proposed €54 billion in spending cuts.

Recent developments

  • September 17, 2026Prime Minister Lecornu acknowledged that the 2026 deficit target would be missed and unveiled the outline of a €54 billion austerity budget for 2027.
  • July 6, 2026France said it would soon launch a maritime security mission in the Strait of Hormuz with Britain, but actual deployment had been delayed as of August.
  • July 2026An appeals court shortened Marine Le Pen's ban from public office, clearing the way for her to run in the 2027 presidential election.
  • June 25, 2026The French navy seized the Russian shadow-fleet tanker Deliver near Sicily, its fifth boarding since September 2025.

What to watch

  • Whether a no-confidence motion passes during October's budget debate and brings down the government
  • The shape of the 2027 presidential race, especially whether the RN candidate is Le Pen or Jordan Bardella, and whether the center unites behind one candidate
  • Whether Europeanizing nuclear deterrence leads to actual deployments or agreements with Germany and others
