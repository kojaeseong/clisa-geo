South Asia

India

Prime Minister Narendra Modi (head of state: President Droupadi Murmu)

Since its four-day air war with Pakistan in May 2025, India has kept up a hard line toward Pakistan and held the Indus Waters Treaty in abeyance. At the same time, it is trying to repair relations with China, hosting the BRICS summit in New Delhi in September 2026 and securing Xi Jinping's first visit to India in seven years, and it patched up its tariff dispute with the United States through a trade agreement in February 2026. Amid the energy crisis set off by the Iran war, it is leaning more heavily on Russian crude and pressing ahead with its policy of strategic autonomy.

Grand strategy

Strategic autonomy: rising as an independent great power that belongs to no bloc.

Must achieve

  • High growth and a manufacturing base (supply chains that replace China)
  • Deterring Pakistan and keeping freedom of action against terrorism
  • Primacy in the Indian Ocean
  • A stable border with China

Can concede

  • The volume of Russian crude purchases
  • Terms of trade with the United States
  • Border management agreements with China

Will never concede

  • Third-party mediation on Kashmir
  • A break in the strategic relationship with Russia
  • Joining a formal alliance

India profits by keeping up relations with the United States (the Quad), Russia (energy and arms), and China (inviting Xi Jinping as BRICS chair) all at once. It is hard-line toward Pakistan but has stabilized the border with China to avoid simultaneous crises on two fronts.

Recent developments

  • September 15, 2026An Indian destroyer (identified as INS Kolkata) and a Pakistani patrol vessel collided in the Arabian Sea, the first direct military contact between the two since May 2025.
  • September 13, 2026BRICS leaders adopted the New Delhi Declaration, criticizing unilateral tariffs and urging restraint on the parties to the Middle East conflict, though without naming the United States directly.
  • September 12, 2026Modi and Xi Jinping met on the sidelines of the BRICS summit in New Delhi and agreed to work toward a fair and reasonable settlement of the border dispute.
  • August 31, 2026The Court of Arbitration constituted under the Indus Waters Treaty, with the Permanent Court of Arbitration (PCA) serving as its registry, ruled that India's decision to hold the treaty "in abeyance" is invalid, but India rejected the court's jurisdiction.
  • August 2026India and China held the 25th round of Special Representatives talks on the boundary question, discussing an "early harvest" in boundary delimitation and stabilizing the Line of Actual Control (LAC).

What to watch

  • Whether the India-Pakistan maritime collision leads to accidental escalation, given the absence of a hotline
  • Progress in boundary delimitation talks with China and how far India opens its economy to China
  • Energy import costs and dependence on Russian crude as the Iran war and the Hormuz blockade drag on
