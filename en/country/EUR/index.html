International actors

EU·NATO

Ursula von der Leyen, president of the European Commission; António Costa, president of the European Council; Kaja Kallas, high representative for foreign affairs and security policy; Mark Rutte, NATO secretary-general

On July 23 the EU adopted its 21st package of sanctions against Russia, freezing the assets of 94 banks and targeting 41 shadow-fleet vessels and crypto-assets, and it is disbursing a €90 billion loan to Ukraine for 2026–27. Yet by early September only €11.55 billion had been paid out, and with Ukraine's Defense Ministry facing a €23 billion funding gap, the idea of tapping frozen Russian assets has resurfaced. Europe is wary of the U.S.-led peace talks and is raising defense spending quickly.

Grand strategy

Defending Europe against Russia's military threat, and building the strength to stand on its own without the United States.

Must achieve

  • Ukraine's survival as a state, which means preventing a Russian victory
  • A surge in defense spending and the rebuilding of its defense industry
  • Keeping sanctions on Russia in place

Can concede

  • The timing of Ukraine's NATO membership
  • Some economic ties with China (to the extent of de-risking)
  • De facto acceptance of the reality of occupation, short of legal recognition

Will never concede

  • Legal recognition of borders changed by force
  • Being shut out of negotiations on Ukraine
  • The credibility of NATO's Article 5

Europe has accepted that the United States is shifting the burden onto it and has begun to rearm; it is responding with a €90 billion loan for Ukraine and a 21st sanctions package against Russia. But decisions come slowly and divisions among member states run deep, so its defensive aim, to avoid being dragged along by U.S.-led negotiations, outweighs any offensive one.

Recent developments

  • September 18, 2026The European Commission disbursed an additional €3.3 billion in defense funding to Ukraine.
  • September 1, 2026The European Commission maintains that €90 billion is enough for now, but Sweden, the Netherlands, Spain and Poland are again pushing to use €210 billion in frozen Russian assets.
  • July 23, 2026The EU adopted its 21st sanctions package against Russia, targeting finance, the shadow fleet, refineries and crypto-assets.
  • June 25, 2026The first €3.2 billion of the €90 billion loan for Ukraine was disbursed.

What to watch

  • A 22nd sanctions package and whether agreement is reached on using frozen Russian assets
  • Whether Europe is shut out of the U.S.-mediated peace talks or given a role in security guarantees
  • The gap in financial support for Ukraine after 2027
