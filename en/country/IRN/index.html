Iran

Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei (succeeded March 2026); President Masoud Pezeshkian

Mojtaba Khamenei succeeded his father, Ali Khamenei, who was killed in U.S.-Israeli airstrikes on February 28, and Iran has now been at war with the United States and Israel for seven months. After a June ceasefire memorandum collapsed in July, Tehran has used its control of the Strait of Hormuz as leverage under a U.S. naval blockade. On September 25 it offered a road map to reopen the strait within seven days, which Trump rejected.

Grand strategy

Survival of the Islamic Republic.

Must achieve

  • Ending the war without the regime collapsing
  • Lifting the blockade and sanctions and recovering frozen assets
  • Preserving nuclear latency (enrichment capability)

Can concede

  • Caps on enrichment levels, or a temporary halt to enrichment
  • Reopening Hormuz to shipping
  • Reining in the Houthis and other proxies

Will never concede

  • Permanently renouncing the right to enrich
  • Terms that would lead to regime change
  • Dismantling its missile force

Though losing militarily, Iran holds Hormuz, the chokepoint of the world's energy supply, as a bargaining chip. It is enduring the blockade and the bombing and playing for time, waiting for rising oil prices and the November midterms to unsettle U.S. domestic politics first.
