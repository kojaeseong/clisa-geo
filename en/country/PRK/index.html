North Korea

Kim Jong Un (general secretary of the Workers' Party of Korea and chairman of the State Affairs Commission)

Power Structures · Kim Jong Un

At its Ninth Party Congress in February 2026, the Workers' Party formally enshrined North Korea's status as a nuclear weapons state and the two-state doctrine, which defines South Korea as a permanently hostile state. Pyongyang supplies Russia with troops, drone operators and missiles in exchange for technology and money, and Xi's visit to Pyongyang in June, his first in seven years, restored ties with China, securing the backing of both Beijing and Moscow at once. Through September, North Korea conducted at least nine missile launches in 2026, firing 15 missiles—a sharp increase over the previous year—and despite Trump's interest in a meeting, it rejects demands for denuclearization as unrealistic.

Grand strategy

The perpetuation of Kim Jong Un's regime, with nuclear-weapons status as its insurance policy.

Must achieve

  • Recognition as a nuclear weapons state (arms-control talks, not denuclearization)
  • Securing patronage from Russia and China at the same time
  • Separation from South Korea (the two-state doctrine)

Can concede

  • A temporary halt to nuclear and ICBM tests
  • The frequency of missile launches
  • The size of troop deployments to Russia

Will never concede

  • Nuclear weapons
  • Opening up the regime
  • Any discussion of unification led by South Korea

North Korea sells troops and weapons to Russia for technology and money, and Xi's visit has restored ties with China as well, securing the backing of both. With Trump, Pyongyang will entertain only a deal that accepts its nuclear status.

  • May 25, 2026North Korea fired ballistic missiles into the Yellow Sea.

What to watch

  • Whether a U.S.–North Korea summit takes place, and the prospects of a deal trading sanctions relief for a nuclear freeze
  • Expanded deployments to Russia (estimates of 30,000 to 50,000) and the military technology transferred in return
  • Whether North Korea resumes testing with a seventh nuclear test or an ICBM (Hwasong-20) launch
