Clisa Geopolitics’ probabilistic forecasts for each issue, with their resolution dates and the reasoning behind them.

Weekly forecast review

Every Monday, forecast probabilities are revisited in light of the previous week’s events. Both revised and unchanged forecasts are listed, with reasons.

October 12 review 4 revised · 2 unchanged

Last week the United States allowed supplies of Russian diesel, but Russia stayed away from a U.S.-led meeting, and Iran kept the strait closed, calling the U.S. reply insufficient. The probabilities of four forecasts were lowered, and two forecasts with conflicting factors were left unchanged.

  • U.S.-Russia-Ukraine technical talks held 50% → 20%
    The October 9 meeting in Miami brought together only the United States, Ukraine, and European representatives; Russia did not attend. The same day, describing the Trump-Putin phone call, the Kremlin relayed President Putin's position that negotiations with Ukraine cannot resume for now. With less than three weeks left before the October 31 deadline and no date or venue announced for three-way talks, the probability that U.S.-Russia-Ukraine technical talks will be held was cut sharply.
  • Full Russia-Ukraine ceasefire takes effect 10% → 8%
    Russian strikes killed at least 28 people across Ukraine on October 7, 33 in Kramatorsk on October 8, and at least 20 in Zaporizhzhia on October 10. With Russia staying away from U.S.-led negotiations, the probability that a full ceasefire signed by both sides takes effect by year-end was lowered further.
  • Full Russia-Ukraine ceasefire holds 30 days 30% (unchanged)
    The probability of a full Russia-Ukraine ceasefire taking effect by year-end was lowered, but this forecast runs to the end of June 2027. Because the United States is pressing Ukraine to accept a ceasefire on Russia's terms, there is still room for a ceasefire, once reached, to hold for more than 30 days, so the probability is left unchanged.
  • Brent below $90 at year-end 30% → 25%
    Member countries of the International Energy Agency agreed on October 7 to speed up stockpile releases, and the United States allowed supplies of Russian diesel on October 9, yet Brent crude closed at $104.72 a barrel on October 9. With tanker attacks spreading north of Qatar (October 7), off the United Arab Emirates (October 9), and to Oman's Sohar anchorage (October 11), the probability of Brent falling below $90 by year-end was lowered.
  • U.S.-Iran ceasefire and Hormuz reopening 25% → 20%
    On October 11, Iran called the U.S. reply insufficient and said it would not open the strait until its conditions are met, and the United States did not accept Iran's proposal for joint management of the strait. The probability that a ceasefire takes effect and the strait's reopening is officially announced within the remaining 11 weeks was lowered.
  • U.S. resumes strikes on mainland Iran 50% (unchanged)
    President Trump said on October 8 that he would not attack Iran before the November 3 midterm elections. But there have been reports of preparations to resume strikes after the election, and costly actions such as enforcing the naval blockade and additional aircraft carrier movements continue. With words and actions pointing in different directions, the probability is left unchanged.
October 5 review 4 revised · 2 unchanged

Last week the United States sent more forces to the Middle East, raising the military pressure on Iran, and North Korea fired a hypersonic intermediate-range missile while rejecting inter-Korean contact. The probabilities of four forecasts were adjusted, and two forecasts with conflicting factors were left unchanged.

  • U.S. resumes strikes on mainland Iran 45% → 50%
    On October 1 the United States sent a carrier strike group and an amphibious ready group to the Middle East, and President Trump said expanded airstrikes were possible after the midterms. Moving forces is a costly step, and it points the same way as the rhetoric, so the probability that the United States resumes airstrikes on mainland Iran has been raised.
  • U.S.-Iran phased agreement signed 30% → 25%
    On October 4, Iran's Foreign Ministry said the latest U.S. proposal still put the nuclear issue first, and the same day Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said Iran would not open the strait until its seven conditions were met. The room for signing a phased U.S.-Iran agreement that separates reopening the strait from the nuclear issue has narrowed, but draft texts are still being exchanged through mediators.
  • U.S.-Iran phased agreement holds 90 days 15% → 12%
    With the probability that a phased agreement is signed now lower, the probability that it holds for 90 days after signing has been lowered in step.
  • Inter-Korean military lines not restored 85% → 90%
    In statements on October 2 and 3, North Korea denied responsibility for the land mine and rejected contact with South Korea, and on October 3 it launched a hypersonic intermediate-range missile. With no North Korean action in response to Seoul's calls for dialogue, the balance has tilted further toward the inter-Korean military communication lines remaining unrestored.
  • Brent below $90 at year-end 30% (unchanged)
    The G7 agreed on October 2 to release 100 million barrels of reserve oil over four months, but with the strait still blockaded and the Houthis claiming an attack on Saudi Aramco facilities, Brent remains above $100 a barrel. With factors pushing both down and up, the probability is left unchanged.
  • U.S.-Russia-Ukraine technical talks held 50% (unchanged)
    President Zelensky said on October 4 that the United States had proposed holding trilateral technical-level talks by the end of October and that Ukraine supports this timetable. Russia has not yet announced agreement on the timing or venue, so the probability is left unchanged.
