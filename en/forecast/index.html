Weekly forecast review

Every Monday, forecast probabilities are revisited in light of the previous week’s events. Both revised and unchanged forecasts are listed, with reasons.

October 5 review 4 revised · 2 unchanged

Last week the United States sent more forces to the Middle East, raising the military pressure on Iran, and North Korea fired a hypersonic intermediate-range missile while rejecting inter-Korean contact. The probabilities of four forecasts were adjusted, and two forecasts with conflicting factors were left unchanged.

  • U.S. resumes strikes on mainland Iran 45% → 50%
    On October 1 the United States sent a carrier strike group and an amphibious ready group to the Middle East, and President Trump said expanded airstrikes were possible after the midterms. Moving forces is a costly step, and it points the same way as the rhetoric, so the probability that the United States resumes airstrikes on mainland Iran has been raised.
  • U.S.-Iran phased agreement signed 30% → 25%
    On October 4, Iran's Foreign Ministry said the latest U.S. proposal still put the nuclear issue first, and the same day Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said Iran would not open the strait until its seven conditions were met. The room for signing a phased U.S.-Iran agreement that separates reopening the strait from the nuclear issue has narrowed, but draft texts are still being exchanged through mediators.
  • U.S.-Iran phased agreement holds 90 days 15% → 12%
    With the probability that a phased agreement is signed now lower, the probability that it holds for 90 days after signing has been lowered in step.
  • Inter-Korean military lines not restored 85% → 90%
    In statements on October 2 and 3, North Korea denied responsibility for the land mine and rejected contact with South Korea, and on October 3 it launched a hypersonic intermediate-range missile. With no North Korean action in response to Seoul's calls for dialogue, the balance has tilted further toward the inter-Korean military communication lines remaining unrestored.
  • Brent below $90 at year-end 30% (unchanged)
    The G7 agreed on October 2 to release 100 million barrels of reserve oil over four months, but with the strait still blockaded and the Houthis claiming an attack on Saudi Aramco facilities, Brent remains above $100 a barrel. With factors pushing both down and up, the probability is left unchanged.
  • U.S.-Russia-Ukraine technical talks held 50% (unchanged)
    President Zelensky said on October 4 that the United States had proposed holding trilateral technical-level talks by the end of October and that Ukraine supports this timetable. Russia has not yet announced agreement on the timing or venue, so the probability is left unchanged.
